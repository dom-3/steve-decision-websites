### The finding, five lines

1. **Exeter House (9635) and The Pines (9636) are two statutory list-numbers for ONE building** — a double-fronted early-C19 Georgian house on King Street. The two entries carry *verbatim identical* Cadw exterior text, and that text names both central doors of a single facade: "Paired entrances to centre; 6-panel door to Exeter House, boarded door to The Pines, blocked opening to right."
2. This is a **genuine one-building/two-numbers case, not the Doc-299 BLB-mirror conflation trap** — established precisely by reading each entry on its own and finding that the shared description itself distinguishes the two halves by their doors.
3. Both halves together were, at the 1986 listing, the premises of **Oriel Evans Bookshop** (named in both entry titles). Present-day trading status was not checked this pass.
4. **Elm House (9637)** is a different building and a different date: a later-C19 Victorian 3-storey-and-basement roughcast front — splayed bays with dentil cornices, wide bracket eaves — set **over a structure with earlier origins shown on the 1834 survey map**, i.e. a Victorian remodelling of a pre-1834 core, not a wholly new build.
5. All three were listed on **25 September 1986 for GROUP VALUE only** — townscape contribution to the continuous King-Street frontage row, not individual special interest; Exeter/Pines sit lower, with Elm House stepped up to the right.

### What each object says

**Exeter House including Oriel Evans Bookshop — Cadw 9635, Grade II, Building Class Commercial.** Listed 25 September 1986. Early C19 (shown on the 1834 survey map). Georgian 3-storey, 3-window cement-render front with later alterations; deep ground-floor architrave and plinth punctuated to the right by pilasters; slate roof, brick end chimney stacks; various sash windows, mostly replaced but with small-pane glazing retained to the 2nd-floor right; a contemporary 5-light shop front with swept-out cornice to the left flanked by pilasters. Paired entrances to the centre — 6-panel door to Exeter House, boarded door to The Pines, blocked opening to the right. Reason for listing: group value. (Wikidata Q29488397.)

**The Pines including Oriel Evans Bookshop — Cadw 9636, Grade II, Building Class Commercial.** Listed 25 September 1986. The exterior description is word-for-word the same as 9635, because it describes the same building; the "History" note adds only "Early C19 (shown on 1834 survey map)." The boarded central door is the one "to The Pines." Reason for listing: group value. (Wikidata Q29488398.)

**Elm House — Cadw 9637, Grade II, Building Class Domestic.** Listed 25 September 1986. "History: Later C19 Victorian." Exterior: 3-storey and basement, 4-window roughcast front with end pilaster strips and plinth, "to structure with earlier origins as shown on 1834 survey map"; slate roof, heightened brick end chimney stacks, wide bracket eaves; tripartite sash glazing and alternate splayed bays with dentil cornices to each floor; boarded door to the left, half-glazed door to the right under a deep panelled door. Reason for listing: group value. Located set in a continuous row of frontages, stepped up from The Pines to the left. (Wikidata Q29488399.)

### Reading notes and traps handled

- **The BLB transcription carries typos.** The mirror's Exeter House copy reads "depp" (for *deep*), "fronages" (*frontages*) and even "Bokshop" (*Bookshop*) in the entry title. These are transcription slips in the mirror, silently corrected above; they are logged in the source Notes so a later reader does not treat "Bokshop" as a genuine name variant.
- **The 1834 survey map** recurs across the Laugharne listings and is the same source identified in Doc 164b. Here it is cited cleanly for both the Georgian pair and the Elm House core — a contrast with the garbled 1834-survey note at the Globe (Doc 198), which was Cadw's own error.

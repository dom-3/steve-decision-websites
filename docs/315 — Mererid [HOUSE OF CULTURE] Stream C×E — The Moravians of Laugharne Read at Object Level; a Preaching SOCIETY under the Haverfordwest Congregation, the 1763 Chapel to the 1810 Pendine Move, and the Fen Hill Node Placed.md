### Five-line finding

1. **Laugharne was a preaching SOCIETY, not a congregation.** Hutton lists it among the four Welsh Moravian *societies* — "In Wales, with Haverfordwest as a centre, they had societies at Laughharne, Fishguard, Carmarthen and Carnarvon." A society/preaching-place was deliberately *not* constituted as a full congregation under the German-directed "United Flocks" policy (Benjamin La Trobe, 1765–86). This distinction is the key that makes the whole record legible.
2. **Haverfordwest was the single Welsh congregation, founded 1763.** The DWB (Gambold family) states that George Gambold (d. 1755) and John Sparks "founded the society which was in 1763 to become the Moravian congregation at Haverfordwest — the only one in Wales," pastored by the Moravian bishop John Gambold (1711–1771). Hutton independently makes Haverfordwest the Welsh centre. **Cross-attested → Verified-strong.**
3. **The Laugharne chapel: 1763 → 1810.** GENUKI (citing the 1851 Religious Census and chapel records) records a "Chapel (Moravian, 1763), Laugharne parish until 1810 then new chapel in Pendine parish (at Tremoilet)"; the Methodists "had apparently assumed possession of this chapel by 1850"; its records are kept "with those of Moravian Chapel (Haverfordwest)" — independently consistent with the DWB's Haverfordwest link.
4. **Membership centred on Laugharne, drift into Methodism.** Lloyd (via GENUKI): the Moravians were "closely intertwined with … Methodism," and "the members were by no means all Laugharne people, for some lived at St Clears, others at Pendine, etc." The 1810 Pendine successor is recorded as "Peter Williams's Memorial English Presbyterian/Moravian chapel" — tying the society to the Peter Williams / Grist Mariner's Chapel material of HoC-282 and evidencing the Moravian→Methodist/Presbyterian slide.
5. **The node placed, not identified; a date tension flagged.** The HoC-307 Fen Hill "Moravian Bishop" tradition and Fernhill Cottage "former Moravian Chapel" now sit against a *documented* Moravian society at Laugharne c.1763–1810 — but no source read attaches those specific buildings to the 1763 chapel. And Hutton's "British Collapse" window (to 1801) sits against the 1810 Pendine continuation: **flagged, not reconciled.**

### The framework: society vs congregation

The single most useful thing this pass establishes is a vocabulary. Under the "United Flocks" system the Brethren deliberately kept most British preaching-places as **societies** — hearers who "for the most part remained members of the Church of England" and "once a quarter, with a Moravian minister at their head, marched in procession to the Communion in the parish church" (Hutton). Only those with a "distinct call to the Brethren's Church" — determined by the Lot — were admitted as full members. Laugharne, Fishguard, Carmarthen and Carnarvon were societies of this kind; Haverfordwest alone in Wales was raised to a **congregation** (1763).

This is why the Laugharne Moravian presence leaves such a faint, ambiguous documentary trace: it was never a self-standing church with its own membership roll, but an outstation of the Haverfordwest congregation. It also explains the denominational blur that Lloyd notes and that the "Presbyterian/Moravian" Pendine dedication records — a society whose hearers were, by design, only loosely Moravian and could pass to the Methodists (as the Laugharne-parish chapel did by 1850) with little friction.

### The Haverfordwest parent and the Gambolds

The Gambold family of Cardiganshire/Pembrokeshire is the human hinge. William Gambold (1672–1728), grammarian and rector of Puncheston, had five sons; the eldest, **John Gambold (1711–1771)**, an "Oxford Methodist" who knew Charles Wesley, met Zinzendorf in 1739, resigned his Oxford living, joined the Brethren in 1744 and became a Moravian bishop in 1753, returning to pastor the Haverfordwest congregation and dying there in 1771 (DWB). His brother **George Gambold (d. 1755)**, a former Methodist exhorter, "continued his brother's school at Haverfordwest and … became a Moravian, founding with John Sparks the society which was in 1763 to become the Moravian congregation at Haverfordwest — the only one in Wales." The bishop is a candidate — no more — for the "Moravian Bishop" of the Fen Hill tradition, but no Laugharne residence is attested for him and the tradition remains un-run.

### The Laugharne chapel and the 1810 Pendine move

GENUKI's Pendine page carries the concrete chapel data, drawn from the 1851 Religious Census and chapel records: a Moravian chapel dated **1763** in **Laugharne parish**, continuing there until **1810**, when a **new chapel was built in Pendine parish at Tremoilet**; by **1850** the Methodists had taken possession; the records are held with the **Haverfordwest** Moravian chapel. Alongside it GENUKI lists "Peter Williams's Memorial English Presbyterian/Moravian chapel, Pendine SN23620811, built 1810, rebuilt 1896" — the 1810 successor, commemorating Peter Williams (1723–1796), whom HoC-282 placed preaching at the Grist Mariner's Chapel after the Moravians took it over. The composite label is not sloppiness on GENUKI's part; it records exactly the tangled Moravian/Methodist/Presbyterian succession that Lloyd describes.

### The date tension (held open, not reconciled)

Hutton's abandonment list sits in a chapter titled **1760–1801**, implying the Laugharne *society* was given up by around the turn of the century. GENUKI/Lewis have the Laugharne-parish *chapel* continuing until the **1810** Pendine move. These are not necessarily in conflict — the most economical reading (untested) is that the **official Moravian society status lapsed** under the United Flocks retreat by c.1801, while the **local chapel/meeting continued** under increasingly Methodist/Presbyterian auspices to 1810. But that is a hypothesis, and per the register's discipline it is **flagged, not asserted.** A spin-off Open thread records the route to settle it (Lloyd at the volume; the Haverfordwest congregation records; the 1851 Religious Census return).

This pass **does not edit** the pre-existing fact recXhOZKERXjpZgIY ("Moravian congregation given up in early-C19 retreat, Hutton"); it only contextualises it — Hutton's chapter is in fact 1760–1801, and the "early-C19" phrasing is left for its owner.

## The question this pass answers

Doc 312's next-target line asked for an object-level identity test of the two post-1900-auction Island House occupiers left as bare stubs in the record: **"Major Schneider" (fl. 1907–08)** and **"Major Congreve" (fl. 1909–10)** — and it flagged a specific trap to guard, the **Congreve-VC near-collision** (the war-hero Congreves, Walter Norris Congreve VC and his son William La Touche Congreve VC).

Those two stubs came from Doc 225, which had already read three independent newspapers at object level and tiered two facts:

- **F225-3 (Verified-strong):** by January 1908 the ownership of Island House had passed to **Thomas Titley**, with **Major Schneider** as occupier/tenant — the house had become a let property under an absentee owner (Carmarthen Journal 6 Mar 1908; Carmarthen Weekly Reporter 13 Mar 1908; Welshman 13 Mar 1908 — "Mr. Thos. Titley, the owner of Island House, Laugharne, and Major Schneider, the occupier of the same").
- **F225-5 (Verified):** **Major Congreve** succeeded Major Schneider as occupier by September 1909 (Carm Journal 3 Sep 1909, flower-show secretary "Major Congreve, of Island House") and was still resident at the December 1910 flood (Carm Journal 23 Dec 1910, "Major Congreave").

The open puzzle was who these two men were, and whether "Congreve" succeeding "Schneider" was a change of tenant at all.

## The finding: one man, not two

**It was one man.** The apparent Schneider → Congreve *succession* is a **surname change**, not a change of occupier. Two independent local-history objects, read in full this pass, converge:

- The **Island House Restoration Ltd owners' history** (islandhouse.wales) states it plainly: under its 1902 entry it heads the occupier "Major Claude Vyvian Congreve (Schneider)", records that "in 1908 it is reported… under the heading *Drainage Scandal* that the owner of Island House was Mr Thomas Titley and the occupier was a Major Schneider, later on in another news article in 1909 it states that a Major Congreve is living at Island House **and they were actually two of the same** as the Major had a double-barrelled name 'Congreve-Schneider'"; the change of styling shows "in around 1909".
- The **Laugharne Lines** trail (the project's own stop page, Steve John's research) records the same occupier as "**Major Congreve (1862-1923)**" who "moved in" in 1903, adding "The Congreves were originally from Germany and **their original name was Schneider**."

The internal logic corroborates it: the F225-3 owner **Thomas Titley** is the occupier's **father-in-law** — the major married Titley's daughter in 1902 (see below). An absentee owner letting the house to his own son-in-law is exactly the owner/occupier split F225-3 recorded, and it dissolves the "succession" of F225-5 into a single continuous tenancy.

So the record should now read **one occupancy, 1903–1923**, under the name Schneider until c.1909 and Congreve thereafter — logged as **F314-1 (Verified)**. This corrects, without deleting, the earlier impression that Schneider was followed by a different tenant Congreve.

## The family: anchored to General Sir J. W. Schneider

The occupier was a **son of General Sir John William Schneider, KCB (1824–1903)**, of the Bombay Infantry/Army. The general is not a scope-marginal invention: he is documented in an **authoritative institutional catalogue** — the **Royal Collection Trust**, RCIN 2500913, "Brigadier General Schneider and staff: Abyssinia Expedition, 1867-8", whose catalogue record describes "Brigadier General JW Schneider of the Bombay Infantry (later Sir General) (1824-1903)." His death in 1903 is corroborated by a Daily Telegraph obituary of 1 June 1903 surfaced in the same search. The general's identity and dates are therefore **Verified** (**F314-2**); the father–son link itself rests on the secondary owners' page plus the probate it quotes, and is held as strong-secondary. The general's widow, **Dame Amelia Euphemia Schneider** (daughter of Colonel Charles James Colley Davidson, Bengal Engineers), is recorded by islandhouse.wales as dying **at Laugharne on 16 March 1915** — a second family tie to the town.

## The forename is not yet settled — and that is the trap

The one thing this pass does **not** resolve, and deliberately does not assert, is the man's **forename**. The single owners' page gives two different answers:

- its heading calls him **"Major Claude Vyvian Congreve (Schneider)" / "Major C. V. Schneider"**; but
- the **1903 National Probate Calendar entry it quotes** for the general's estate names the executor son as **"Stewart Melville Congreve Schneider, major in H.M. Army."**

The probate reading is the more primary-derived, and it carries an important detail: **"Congreve" was already a name-element in the major's name in 1903** — before the c.1909 styling change — which means the c.1909 shift was simply **dropping "Schneider" and using the existing family name "Congreve" as the surname.** But a general may have had more than one major son, so "Stewart Melville Congreve Schneider" being the 1903 executor does **not** by itself prove he is the Island House man. The forename is logged **Referenced-pending (F314-3)** and left for a primary read — the occupier's own **1923 probate** (place of death: Island House) or a name-change instrument would settle it. This is exactly the kind of near-name-match that has contaminated the record before, so it is held open rather than guessed.

## The Congreve-VC near-collision: guarded and refuted

The target's flagged trap is dispatched. The Island House "Major Congreve" is **not** of the **Walter Norris Congreve VC / William La Touche Congreve VC** line; any such identification is **unsourced**. The Congreve surname here is the **family's own name-element** — present as "…Congreve Schneider" in the 1903 probate — adopted as the surname c.1909 in place of Schneider, and reinforced by the marriage into the Titley/Congreve family. Logged **Refuted (F314-4)**: the VC-family *claim* is disproved, guarding against a shared-surname false identification.

## Supporting detail (secondary, held pending)

- **Occupancy and death (F314-5, Referenced-pending):** resident from 1903; **died at Island House on 17 February 1923**. His son **John, born at Island House in 1912, died on WW2 service in 1942** (Laugharne Lines). The 1903 arrival aligns with Doc 216 (Capt. Thomas Jones bought the freehold at the 1900 auction and let the house) and with F225-3 (Titley owner by 1908). The son's death is CWGC-checkable, but that edges into the war-memorial work already handled by the other branch (Doc 304) and was not chased here.
- **The Titley marriage (F314-6, Referenced-pending):** the major married **Mary Hudson (née Titley)** in 1902, a daughter of **Thomas Titley of Leeds**; Titley — the father-in-law — is the "Thomas Titley, owner of Island House" of F225-3. This resolves the owner/occupier split: the absentee owner let the house to his own son-in-law.
- **German-origin tradition (F314-7, Tradition):** Laugharne Lines holds the family "originally from Germany", the original surname Schneider (German for "tailor"). Recorded as tradition only; the general's documented Bombay-Army career says nothing of it.
- **Household colour:** an artistic household — the major played banjo and sang, his wife played piano, both were skilled watercolourists, with rehearsals and soirées into the 1930s; he was a friend from Indian-Army days of **Colonel Bolton of Elm House** and had previously lived at **Fullerton** (Laugharne Lines).

## Sourcing and tiering discipline

Three objects were read directly this pass: the islandhouse.wales owners' page and the laugharnelines.wales stop (both fetched in full), and the RCT catalogue record (read at page-metadata level; the page body is client-rendered). Both Welsh pages are **secondary/compiled** local history and are tiered accordingly — the identity-resolution (F314-1) reaches **Verified** only because two *independent* secondary sources agree **and** the Titley father-in-law logic corroborates; the forename and the biographical details remain **Referenced-pending** or **Tradition**. Nothing here is court-grade on names. The one primary-anchored fact is the general's identity via the RCT catalogue.

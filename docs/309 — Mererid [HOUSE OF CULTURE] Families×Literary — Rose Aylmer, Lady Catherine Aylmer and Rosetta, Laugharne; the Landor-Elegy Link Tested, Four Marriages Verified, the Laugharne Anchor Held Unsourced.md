## 0. The claim under test

Laugharne Lines' **Rosetta** stop page (laugharnelines.wales/htm-docs/stops/rosetta.htm, © 2020) makes two linked claims that would give the town a share in one of the best-known short elegies in English:

> "The house was once owned by Lady Catherine Aylmer, a colourful character who married 4 times. She was tried for adultery in 1772, then married the disreputable Baron Henry Aylmer 4th Lord of Balrath with whom she had a daughter Rose, before marrying a Thomas Price of Laugharne. Rose was the subject of the famous love poem, 'Rose Aylmer', written by Walter Savage Landor (1775–1864 … who lived in nearby Tenby in the 1790s). Sadly Rose died of cholera in India in 1800, aged just 21."

The page thus asserts (a) that **Rosetta was owned by Lady Catherine Aylmer**; (b) a biography of Lady Catherine — four marriages, a 1772 adultery trial, marriage to Baron Henry Aylmer, then to **"a Thomas Price of Laugharne"**; and (c) that her daughter **Rose Aylmer** was Landor's subject, Landor being placed **"in nearby Tenby."**

This run tests every checkable element at object level. The result is a *split verdict*: the human biography is largely **Verified** against the Complete Peerage; the two claims that actually tie the story to **Laugharne** — "Thomas Price of Laugharne" and the Rosetta ownership — are **not** corroborated and are held **Unsourced / near-collision open**.

## 2. Rose Whitworth Aylmer — VERIFIED

From the **Dictionary of Indian Biography** (object-read) and the **Complete Peerage** (via digest of vol. I p.369):

- **Born October 1779**, the **only daughter** of **Henry Aylmer, 4th Baron Aylmer**, and his wife **Catharine, née Whitworth** — Catharine being **sister of Charles Whitworth** (later 1st Earl Whitworth, the ambassador sent to Napoleon in 1803). Rose's middle name *Whitworth* is her mother's family name.
- She **went out to India in 1798** (the DIB misprints this as "1878") with her aunt (a Whitworth), wife of Sir Henry Russell, puisne judge and later Chief Justice of Bengal; she became engaged to Sir Henry's son (afterwards 2nd baronet).
- She **died of cholera on 2 March 1800** at her uncle's house in **Calcutta**, and was **buried in the South Park Street cemetery**, the tomb inscription taken from Young's *Night Thoughts* (iii. 70).
- Siblings included **Gen. Sir Matthew Whitworth-Aylmer, 5th Baron Aylmer** (Governor of Lower Canada) and **Adm. Sir Frederick Whitworth William Aylmer, 6th Baron**.

**Correction to the claim (age).** Born Oct 1779, died 2 March 1800 → Rose was **20**, not 21 (she would have turned 21 only that October). Tier: **Verified** (from the recorded dates).

**Correction to the claim (cause).** The DIB gives **cholera**. The popular "died of a surfeit of pineapples" story is later colour and is **Tradition/legend**, not the biographical record.

## 3. Landor and the elegy — VERIFIED (with the geography corrected to Swansea)

The **Dictionary of Indian Biography** states plainly that **"Walter Savage Landor wrote verses to her at Swansea about 1796–7, and she lent him the book which suggested the subject of his poem *Gebir*"** (Clara Reeve's romance); **"Landor's elegy on her death was published in 1806."**

So the Rose Aylmer acquaintance is anchored by the authoritative reference to **Swansea**, in the years **c.1796–7**, on the Welsh coast — **not to Laugharne, and not (specifically) to Tenby.** Landor's wider Welsh-coast residence of those years did take in **both Tenby and Swansea** (the DNB detail, held Referenced-pending here), and it was in that circle that he "made friends with the family of Lord Aylmer." Laugharne Lines' "in nearby Tenby" is therefore a *defensible half-truth about Landor's whereabouts* but **misleading about the Rose connection**, which the record places at **Swansea**. The trail must not let a reader infer that Landor courted Rose *at Laugharne*: he did not.

The elegy itself (published 1806; public domain):

> Ah, what avails the sceptred race!
> Ah, what the form divine!
> What every virtue, every grace!
> Rose Aylmer, all were thine.
>
> Rose Aylmer, whom these wakeful eyes
> May weep, but never see,
> A night of memories and of sighs
> I consecrate to thee.

*(The Laugharne Lines transcription garbles three words — "sceptre race" for "sceptred race", "those" for "these", and drops the second "of" in "memories and of sighs". Minor, but worth fixing if the stanza is ever quoted on the trail.)*

## 4. Lady Catherine (Catharine) Aylmer née Whitworth — the human biography VERIFIED

From the **Complete Peerage vol. I p.369** (and Burke's *Extinct Peerage* p.582), object-read via the faithful digest — **Catharine Whitworth** (d. 9 January 1805, Middle Hill, Somerset), daughter of **Sir Charles Whitworth MP** (of Leybourne, Kent, d. 1778) and **Martha Shelley**:

| # | Husband | Date | Place (as recorded) |
|---|---------|------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Philip Cade** | 17 Feb 1761 | — |
| — | *divorced from Cade* | **5 Dec 1772** | *"with her association with Lord Aylmer used as grounds for the divorce"* |
| 2 | **Sir Henry Aylmer, 4th Lord Aylmer** | 23 Feb 1774 | Didbrook, Gloucestershire |
| 3 | **Howell Price** | 22 Sep 1787 | **"Loughurn, County Armagh, Ireland"** |
| 4 | **"unknown" Bowles** | after 1787 | — |

This **corroborates the spine of the Laugharne Lines biography**:

- **"married 4 times"** — VERIFIED (Cade, Aylmer, Price, Bowles).
- **"tried for adultery in 1772"** — VERIFIED in substance: her **divorce from Philip Cade on 5 Dec 1772 was granted on the ground of her adultery with Lord Aylmer** (the ecclesiastical-court / Parliamentary-divorce mechanism of the day), whom she then married in 1774.
- **"the disreputable Baron Henry Aylmer, 4th Lord of Balrath"** — supported: the 4th Lord Aylmer (b. c.1750, d. 22 Oct 1785) was **4th Baron Aylmer of Balrath, co. Meath [I., 1718]** and 7th Baronet [I., 1662], and in **1782 was recommended as "an object of His Majesty's bounty," pleading he was "totally unable to support the dignity of the Peerage"** — an impoverished, embarrassed Irish peer, which fits "disreputable."
- **"with whom she had a daughter Rose"** — VERIFIED (§2).

Tier for §4 as a block: **Verified** (Complete Peerage / Burke's).

## 5. The Laugharne anchor — HELD UNSOURCED / NEAR-COLLISION OPEN

The two claims that make Rosetta a *Laugharne* story do **not** survive the peerage:

**(a) "before marrying a Thomas Price of Laugharne."** The Complete Peerage records the third husband as **Howell Price**, married **22 September 1787**, at a place given as **"Loughurn, County Armagh, Ireland."** Two divergences from the Laugharne Lines claim:

- **Forename:** the record says **Howell** Price, not **Thomas** Price. (Both are Welsh names; "Howell/Hywel" is emphatically so.)
- **Place:** the record's **"Loughurn"** is tagged by the peerage to **County Armagh, Ireland** (geocoded to ~54.32°N, Northern Ireland), **not** to **Laugharne, Carmarthenshire** (~51.77°N). Given the Aylmers were an **Irish** peerage (Balrath, co. Meath), an Irish marriage place is internally plausible.

This is a textbook **near-collision**: an orthographically similar place-name ("Loughurn" ↔ "Laugharne") and a shared surname (Price) sit either side of a one-town claim. **Do not reconcile by assumption in either direction.** It is *possible* the peerage's "Loughurn, Co. Armagh" is itself a misreading of a Welsh **Laugharne** (a Welsh-named groom, Howell Price, would fit a Welsh venue) — but it is equally possible Laugharne Lines has grafted the town onto an Irish marriage and swapped the forename. On the present evidence the **"Thomas Price of Laugharne"** identification is **Unsourced** (forename refuted by the record; place unresolved).

**(b) "Rosetta was once owned by Lady Catherine Aylmer."** No independent object corroborates this: it rests on **Laugharne Lines alone** (a 2020 community-heritage page, not a deed, directory, tithe apportionment or press notice). Tier: **Tradition** until a title/occupancy object (deeds, the 1846 tithe apportionment, or a directory naming her at Rosetta) is read. It cannot go to the public trail as fact.

**Net:** the *person* Lady Catherine Aylmer is real and richly documented; her *Laugharne residence and third-husband-of-Laugharne* — the only things that put Rosetta in the frame — are **not yet sourced**. Reporting this gap is the correct outcome, not a failure: the trail may safely tell the Aylmer/Landor story as a **Swansea/Welsh-coast** literary tale with a **claimed, unverified** Laugharne link to Rosetta, clearly labelled.

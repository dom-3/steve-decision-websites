## Why this thread

Three public authorities give three incompatible numberings for the same family of Laugharne lords, all of them called **Guy de Brian**:

- **Coflein** calls the charter-granter *"Guy De Brian IV … first of the De Brian lords."*
- **Laugharne Community Council** has *Guy IV* acquiring Laugharne in 1247, captured and ransomed 1258, dying 1268 — and his son *Guy V* as the probable granter of the charter.
- **The Corporation website** uses a third scheme: *Guy 1 (1215–1268), Guy 2 (1251–1307), Guy 3 (1280–1349), Guy 4 (1311–1390).*

Because the family reused the forename "Guy" for at least five successive generations, these ordinal numbers are the single biggest source of confusion in the medieval lordship material, and they are exactly why "which Guy granted the charter?" reads as unanswerable. Our own register had inherited the mess: it carries a "Guy de Brian IV" and "Guy de Brian V" (from the Community Council scheme) **alongside** a "Guy (A) grandfather", "Guy (B) d.1307" and "Guy (C) heir" (from the 1307 inquisition) — parallel labels for what are, in places, the same men.

This run does not try to impose a fourth numbering. It reads the two authoritative objects, fixes what the record actually fixes, and shows precisely where the public schemes have and have not earned their precision.

## Objects read at object level

1. **Calendar of Inquisitions post mortem, vol. 5 (Edward II), no. 64** — the 1307 IPM of Guy de Brian, lord of the barony of "Tallauhern" (Laugharne). Read in full via Claude-in-Chrome same-origin fetch of British History Online (`/inquis-post-mortem/vol5/pp31-38`), rate-limit-independent. **Primary, T1.**
2. **The Complete Peerage, vol. 2, "BRYAN or BRIENE", pp. 361–363** — the barony created by writ of 1350. Read direct from the archive.org full text (`completepeerageo02coka`), cross-read against the medievalgenealogy.org.uk corrections page (which patches the article from vol. 14 and cites the 1349 IPM, CIPM vol. 9 no. 333). **Authoritative secondary, T2.**

## What the 1307 IPM says (verbatim anchors)

Heading: **"GUY DE BRIAN alias DE BRIONA, DE BRYON."**

Writ "to the justice of West Wales, dated at Carlisle under the privy seal which the king is using before taking up the government, **26 July, 1 Edw. II**" — i.e. the weeks just after Edward II's accession (7 July 1307). Carmarthen inquisition taken **26 Aug. 1 Edw. II**.

> "**Tallauhern.** The barony (extent given), including a castle … containing 12¼ knight's fees, held of the king in chief by homage and service of 2 knight's fees … for three days at his own charges at the summons of the king's bailiffs of Carmarthen."

The two genealogical sentences that do the real work:

> "Of all which **Joan late the wife of Guy de Brian, grandfather of Guy who now is**, has a third part in dower."

> "**Guy his son, aged 23, is his next heir.**"

The same IPM records the family's other baronies: **Walwyn's Castle** (Pembroke), held of the earl of Gloucester, and — from the Complete Peerage — **Tor Brian** in Devon, the family's Devon caput. Laugharne was one of several baronies the de Brians held, not their only seat.

## The documented spine

Reading the 1307 statement plainly — decedent, decedent's son and heir (aged 23), and the decedent's *father* (the "grandfather of Guy who now is", whose widow Joan still held dower) — gives three named Guy generations from a single primary object. Adding the Complete Peerage barony article extends the line forward:

| Gen. | Who | Fixed by the record | Death |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guy de Brian, husband of **Joan** (she still in dower 1307); *grandfather* of the 1307 heir | 1307 IPM | d. before 1307 |
| 2 | Guy de Brian, **lord who died 1307** | 1307 IPM (writ 26 July 1 Edw. II) | **1307** |
| 3 | Guy de Brian, **heir aged 23 in 1307** (b. c.1283/84); of Walwyn's Castle & Tor Brian | 1307 IPM + CIPM vol. 9 no. 333 | **1349** |
| 4 | Sir **Guy de Bryan, KG**, 1st Baron Bryan (writ 1350); suc. father 1349 aged 30+ | Complete Peerage vol. 2 | **17 Aug 1390** |
| 5 | Guy de Bryan (jr), heir-apparent, **d. vitâ patris** (will 1383) | Complete Peerage vol. 2 | **1386** |

On the KG's death in 1390 the barony fell (by modern doctrine) into abeyance between his two granddaughters and coheirs — his son Guy having predeceased him in 1386. (Gen. 4 and 5 are the subject of Doc 288; this run confirms them from the same article and links them to the earlier spine.)

## The concordance — where each public scheme lands

The record fixes a firm **death-date spine: a Guy who died in 1307, a Guy who died in 1349, a Guy who died in 1390.** Against that:

- **The Corporation's death years are right.** Its "Guy 2 …1307", "Guy 3 …1349", "Guy 4 …1390" match the IPM/CP spine exactly (Gens 2, 3, 4). Its *birth* years and its "Guy 1 (1215–1268)" are the parts that rest on no cited primary evidence.
- **The Community Council** compresses the pre-1307 generations into "Guy IV (d.1268)" and "Guy V", and picks Guy V as granter — a guess the documents neither confirm nor deny.
- **Coflein's "Guy IV, first of the lords"** is a third ordinal again, incompatible with both.

**The honest conclusion: no absolute ordinal numbering can be recovered from the open record.** The family reused "Guy", and the descent before 1307 is only partially documented (the 1307 IPM reaches back one generation, to the grandfather and his widow Joan, and no further open object read here fixes an earlier one). Public copy should therefore lean on the **death-date spine** and the **1307 three-generation statement**, never on a numeral. This is the finding that actually unblocks the charter threads: they were stalled because every source imposed a false precision the evidence does not carry.

## The charter grantor (kept honest)

The borough charter is **late-thirteenth-century and undated** (register entity; Adcock 2024). In that generation the lord was **either the Guy who died in 1307 or his father** (Joan's husband). The record does not, without the charter's own text, decide which — so the grantor's identity is logged **Referenced-pending**, not attached to any numbered "Guy". Pinning it needs the charter itself (the Alcwyn C. Evans translations at NLW, archive-gated).

*(Guardrail note: the "1307" throughout is a death / IPM date. It is **not** the charter date and must never be printed as one — the charter stays "late thirteenth century".)*

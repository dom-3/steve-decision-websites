### Fen Hill, The Butts (Cadw 9622)

**Designation.** Grade II; listed 25 September 1986 (the 1986 Laugharne sweep); Building Class Domestic; Cadw Source ID 9622; OS SN299108. Built into the hillside with views over the lower town to the south-east, reached by a steep curved drive entered between rubble gate piers and iron gates. *(Verified — Cadw statutory record.)*

**Build and builder.** "Built 1827 to 1830 by **Dennis Fairchild, an amateur artist**; extension to SW end dated 1865." The 1865 date is carved to the gable of the entrance/south-west front. The listing states the build years and the builder as fact, so both are carried Verified; **Fairchild himself** is carried only Referenced-pending, as no independent biographical source (directory, newspaper, probate) was located this run. He is nonetheless a named early-C19 Laugharne builder-resident worth chasing. *(Build/date Verified; Fairchild Referenced-pending.)*

**Fabric.** A **Regency** two-storey **stucco** double-pile villa on a **U-plan** — Cadw compares the plan to Parson's Lodge and Mapsland. Hipped slate roofs with a gabled extension to the left and brick chimney stacks; a three-window main front with the left bay set back; recessed 12-pane sash windows; french windows to the ground floor with diamond-glazed fanlights and matching timber detail to a trellised verandah. The two-window entrance front to the left is dated 1865 to its gable, with a modern pitched-roof porch to an arched doorway. The original rear range is set back to the left; the blind rubble end wall of the cross range is set back to the right with a lean-to at the north-east end. Internally the house **retains an unusual water system, the basement collecting rainwater to be pumped up**. *(Verified — read verbatim from the listing's exterior and interior descriptions.)*

**Moravian tradition.** Cadw records that Fen Hill was "**later said to have become the home of a Moravian Bishop**, a denomination that had a relatively large following in this area." Cadw hedges with "said to," so this is held at **Tradition**: no bishop is named, and the observation about the Moravian following is Cadw's own.

**1644 siege site-claim.** The listing opens: "Historically one of the sites **from which Laugharne Castle may have been bombarded** in the October/November 1644 Parliamentarian siege." Cadw hedges "may have been," so this too is **Tradition**. Two cautions: (a) the villa itself dates only from 1827–30, so the claim concerns the **hilltop site**, not the building; and (b) Cadw dates the siege "October/November 1644," whereas the town's own standing description is a siege of about a week at the **end of October 1644** — a minor date-spread, flagged here and deliberately **not reconciled** by assumption.

### Fernhill Cottage (Cadw 9626)

**Designation.** Grade II; listed 25 September 1986; Building Class Domestic; Cadw Source ID 9626; OS SN299108 (a few metres north-east of, and below, Fen Hill). **Reason for listing: GROUP VALUE** — it is protected for its relationship to Fen Hill, not for standalone interest. Located above the road to the north-east and below Fern Hill, reached by a track running north off The Butts. A modern extension to the right end is expressly "not of special interest." *(Verified — Cadw statutory record.)*

**History.** "**Formerly the Gardener's Cottage to Fern Hill also known to have been used as a Moravian Chapel. Probably contemporary with Fern Hill (1827 to 1830), shown on 1834 survey map; later C19 alterations when converted to chapel use.**" The 1834 survey map fixes the cottage as pre-1834; the chapel use is Cadw's own statement. *(Verified — read verbatim.)*

**Fabric.** Plain **Gothic** whitewashed rubble; a one-storey-and-attic, three-window front, with the central bay advanced to form a two-storey porch; slate roof; diagonally set brick end chimney stacks; pitched roofs with bargeboards to the attic windows; casement windows to the outer bays; an **acutely pointed entrance arch** and niches to the porch sides; a half-glazed door. The Gothic detailing suits both the picturesque gardener's-cottage origin and the later chapel use. *(Verified. BLB typo "nicnes" read as "niches.")*

### The two together — a small Moravian node

Read as a pair, the two records describe a **Moravian node** on the town's edge: the villa reputedly a Moravian Bishop's home, its cottage a former Moravian Chapel. The **Moravians are not among the six nonconformist causes** enumerated in Doc 213's RCAHMW chapel survey of Laugharne, which makes this a **distinct, hitherto-unlogged strand** of the town's chapel map. It is carried Referenced-pending as a synthesis of the two Cadw records, and flagged as worth a dedicated Moravian-congregation pass (a Moravian church would have kept membership and preaching-place records that a newspaper or denominational history could confirm).

---

### The guardrail catch — the Dylan "Fern Hill" near-collision, held REFUTED

Because Cadw's cottage listing itself calls the villa "**Fern Hill**," this pair sits one letter away from one of the most famous place-names in Welsh literature — **Dylan Thomas's poem "Fern Hill" (1945)**. That is a **name near-collision of exactly the Beynon/Eynon shape** the branch's traps warn about, and it was held refuted before any research began.

The poem's "Fern Hill" is **Fernhill farm at Llangain**, on the Llansteffan peninsula north of Carmarthen — the farm of Thomas's aunt **Ann (Annie) Jones** and her husband Jim Jones, where the poet holidayed as a child in the 1920s. It is a **separate Grade II building roughly fifteen miles from Laugharne** and has nothing to do with the Laugharne villa on The Butts. (Sources: Wikipedia "Fern Hill"; the DylanThomas.com West-Wales trail; Nation.Cymru.)

So the record is explicit: **the Laugharne Fen Hill / Fern Hill (LB 9622) is NOT a Dylan Thomas site.** This follows the branch's standing line without hedging — Dylan's Laugharne pub was Brown's; Island House is not a Dylan site; and this hillside villa is not one either. Reporting the collision as refuted is the intended outcome, not a failure.

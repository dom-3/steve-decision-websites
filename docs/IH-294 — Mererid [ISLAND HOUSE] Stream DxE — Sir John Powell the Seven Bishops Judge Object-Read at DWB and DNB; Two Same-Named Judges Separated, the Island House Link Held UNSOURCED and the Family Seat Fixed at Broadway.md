## What the objects say

**The Welsh judge (DWB, 1633–1696).** Born 1633, son of John Powell of **Pentre Meurig, Llanwrda, Carmarthenshire**; tutored as a boy by Jeremy Taylor; Gray's Inn 1650; Recorder of Brecon 1683–90; Puisne Justice of Brecknock 1685–6; knighted and Judge of Common Pleas 1686, removed to King's Bench 1687. **In June 1688 he was one of the judges who acquitted the Seven Bishops, was dismissed by James II, and restored in 1689.** He **died 7 September 1696 and was buried in Laugharne parish church.** The article states plainly: *"He has been confused with Sir John Powell of Gloucester (1645–1713; D.N.B.)."* His son **Thomas (1664–1720), of Broadway**, Carmarthenshire and Coldbrook, Monmouthshire, was the baronet.

The DWB gives the judge **no residence at Laugharne**. His only Laugharne tie in the object is **burial** in the parish church; the family's Laugharne **seat** named anywhere is his son's — **Broadway** (corroborated by the History of Parliament, read at Doc 172, where the father is styled "of Pentre Meyrick, Llanwrda, Carm. **and** Broadway").

**The Gloucester judge (DNB, 1645–1713).** Born 1645 at Gloucester, son of a Gloucester citizen (mayor 1663) of a Herefordshire family. *"He was not related to either of the contemporary judges of the same name."* Inner Temple 1664, bar 1671; town clerk of Gloucester and its MP 1685; made a serjeant after the 1688 Revolution and **raised to the bench only in 1691** (Exchequer, with knighthood); common pleas 1695; queen's bench 1702 — the Ashby v. White case (1704); the humane witchcraft remark in Jane Wenham's case, *"there is no law against flying"*; described by Swift to Stella (1711). He **died 14 June 1713 at Gloucester**, has a **monument in Gloucester Cathedral**, and was **unmarried**. No Welsh, Laugharne or Island House content of any kind.

## Finding (five lines)

1. **Two men, and the object literature separates them from both sides.** DWB flags the Welsh judge as "confused with Sir John Powell of Gloucester (1645–1713)"; DNB flags the Gloucester judge as "not related to either of the contemporary judges of the same name." The trap is now disarmed at object level from *both* ends.
2. **Only the Welsh Powell (1633–1696) was the actual Seven Bishops judge.** The Gloucester Powell reached the bench only in **1691** and so cannot have sat at the **June 1688** trial; his DNB life records Ashby v. White and the "flying" quip, never the Seven Bishops.
3. **The Welsh judge has no documented Island House link — nor any Laugharne residence at all.** His object-level Laugharne ties are **burial** in the parish church and his son's **1720 monument** there (Doc 172). The family's Laugharne **seat was Broadway**, not Island House.
4. **The "Powell of Island House" association is therefore UNSOURCED as to the house.** It rests on (i) the general Powell-of-Laugharne fame that in fact attaches to Broadway and the parish church, and (ii) the two-judge name confusion. This also confirms Doc 172's finding that the register's earlier **"(of the Castle)"** epithet is unsupported — neither Castle nor Island House is the judge's documented seat.
5. **No onward desk step.** The house-link is a clean negative; any residual "Powell at Island House" is an occupancy-spine surname coincidence already tested via TNA Discovery at Doc 203.

## Verdict

The thread question is answered and closed: **there is no object-level evidence that Sir John Powell — either the Welsh Seven Bishops judge or the Gloucester namesake — was linked to Island House.** The Powell family's real Laugharne anchors are Broadway (seat) and St Martin's (burial and the 1720 monument). The claim is held **UNSOURCED as to the house** — a documented negative, not a failure.

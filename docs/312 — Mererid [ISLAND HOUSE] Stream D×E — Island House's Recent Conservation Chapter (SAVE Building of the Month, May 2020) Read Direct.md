3. **The condition snapshot at handover (Verified).** "Due to the extent of the dereliction of the house, with one of the ranges suffering a collapsed roof, their first task is carrying out emergency repairs to stabilise the structure." This records the structural emergency at the 2020 handover — a collapsed roof on one range — rather than any restored state, and should be read as a 2020 condition note, not a durable description. *(Fact recUiEN9Uk3g8BzKC.)*

4. **The neglect mechanism (Referenced-pending).** SAVE attributes the "descent into dereliction over the last 15 or so years" (i.e. from roughly 2005) to a specific transmission failure: "We understand that the previous owner had bequeathed the house to a number of beneficiaries who never took up responsibility for it." This is the most genuinely additive item — it names the **mechanism** behind the late-C20/early-C21 neglect chapter of the occupancy spine (a bequest to multiple beneficiaries, none of whom acted). But SAVE explicitly hedges it ("We understand that…"), and it rests on this single secondary source, so it is logged **Referenced-pending**, not fact. The natural corroboration route is the probate record for the previous owner and the Companies-House / title history — an archive/registry task, not a desk read. *(Fact recvPuZFddYKaI3V9.)*

5. **The community campaign (context).** SAVE records that "a number of community groups including the Community Council, the History Society and the community newsletter have publicised its plight and they have played a crucial role in calling for it to be rescued," and that Carmarthenshire County Council, Cadw and the SPAB were all involved at various stages. Logged as context within this doc rather than as a separate fact.

### The trap caught — "damaged by cannon fire by Cromwell's troops … in 1644"

The essay repeats the popular claim that Island House "has experienced many changes including being damaged by cannon fire by Cromwell's troops when they attacked the castle in 1644." **Two problems, both flagged; the claim is held at Tradition** *(Fact rec1o0QQmnUUvu7TV):*

- **"Cromwell's troops" is a popular inaccuracy.** The siege of Laugharne Castle at the end of October 1644 (lasting six days / about a week) was a **Parliamentarian force under Major-General Rowland Laugharne besieging the Royalist-held castle** — not Cromwell, who was not present at Laugharne in 1644. House style: describe it as the six-day / week-long siege at the end of October 1644, and do **not** repeat "Cromwell". (Standing guardrail: the town is not named after Rowland Laugharne, and the siege was not "five days".)
- **That Island House *itself* sustained 1644 cannon damage is uncorroborated by the statutory record.** The claim is a secondary/advocacy assertion; it is **not** stated in the Cadw 9671 listing text already read in the register. Until a primary or statutory source corroborates cannon damage to the house's own fabric, the specific claim stays Tradition/uncorroborated — a good, honest outcome, not a failure.

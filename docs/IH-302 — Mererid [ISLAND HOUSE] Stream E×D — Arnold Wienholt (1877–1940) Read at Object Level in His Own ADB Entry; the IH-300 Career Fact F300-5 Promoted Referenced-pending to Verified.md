## What the object says (ADB `wienholt-arnold-9093`, Greville, Vol. 12, 1990)

- **Birth & parentage.** Born 25 November 1877 at Goomburra station, near Allora, Queensland; eldest son of **Edward Wienholt** (pastoralist; separate ADB life `-4956`) and his Victorian-born wife **Ellen, née Williams**. Educated in England at **Eton**; returned to Australia into the pastoral industry. (Consistent with Doc 296's "three sons and three daughters, including Arnold.")
- **Boer War.** Enlisted in the 4th (Queensland Imperial Bushmen) Contingent; embarked for South Africa 18 May 1900; promoted sergeant in June; the contingent served twelve months and was disbanded in Brisbane on 10 August 1901.
- **Pastoral career.** In 1908 became manager of **Wienholt Estates Co. of Australasia Ltd**, responsible for the firm's Queensland properties; a noted cattleman who published a method against tick.
- **First political stint & the lion.** Member for **Fassifern** in the Queensland Legislative Assembly **1909–1913**; having failed to win the federal seat of **Wide Bay** in 1913, went on safari to German South-West Africa (Namibia), where a wounded lion mauled him and he lost full use of his right hand.
- **First World War.** Made his way to Rhodesia; his offer to scout was refused; sailed for Africa March 1915; served in the **British South Africa Police**, then the **East Africa Mounted Rifles**; seconded to the **Intelligence Branch** (warrant officer). Led a patrol into German East Africa (Tanzania), was **wounded and captured on 1 July 1916**, escaped six months later and spent fifteen days crossing unfamiliar country to regain his lines — awarded the **Military Cross**. Further reconnaissance won him the **DSO (October 1918)**; a **bar to the MC** was gazetted in September; promoted **captain**.
- **Marriage & federal politics.** Married **Enid Frances Sydney Jones** on 29 April 1919 at St Philip's Anglican Church, Sydney; home at Washpool Farm, near Kalbar, Qld. Elected to the House of Representatives as **National Party member for Moreton (1919)**; urged repeal of wartime restrictions on German settlers; spoke against public indebtedness and state enterprise; did not contest the 1922 election.
- **Second state stint & authorship.** Returned to the Assembly for **Fassifern in 1930**, retained the seat in 1932, did not contest 1935. Published *The Story of a Lion Hunt* (London, 1922); went lion-hunting on four occasions between 1923 and 1929.
- **Ethiopia and death.** Arrived Addis Ababa December 1935 as war-correspondent for the Brisbane *Courier Mail*; two months later joined the **Ethiopian Red Cross** as a transport officer; recorded his experiences in *The Africans' Last Stronghold* (1938). On the outbreak of WWII sailed to Aden; learned Arabic and Amharic; ordered on 31 August 1940 to proceed overland from the Sudan to Ethiopia with a small party as part of **Military Mission 101** (fostering rebellion against Italy). **Ambushed and wounded, probably on the morning of 10 September 1940, last seen scrambling into the bush and presumed to have died.**
- **Character & estate.** Never smoked or drank alcohol; taciturn but liked a good yarn; "ruthless integrity and exacting truthfulness"; an "uncompromising individualist" with affection for the peoples of Africa. His wife survived him, as did their only daughter **Anne**, who became a prominent artist. His Queensland estate was sworn for probate at **£174,978**.

Island House

A surveying system that pays for itself every week

360° camera walk → 3D model + a fixed nine-lens weekly building survey. One walk of the building becomes a full multi-discipline condition report — automatically, to the same standard, every week, so nothing about a Grade II* historic building's condition slips.
Plus the website is now live on our own platform, off WordPress, fully owned.
Impact: replaces ~£1,000/month (£12,000/yr) of survey software and the surveyor/architect/builder time for a whole-building condition survey — £3k–£8k for a single professional pass, realistically tens of £000s/yr to do weekly by hand. Website: ~£3k–£6k agency migration avoided + ongoing hosting/maintenance saved. A fraction of the time; recurring value.
The Globe

A content engine you can trust — and an event that makes money

Writes, brands, quality-checks and (once switched on) auto-posts to Instagram & Facebook — with an accuracy gate that caught the AI inventing a product we don't sell (“cheesecake”) before it went public.
The Estuary Table (29 Aug) — a zero-budget, food-led bank-holiday relaunch night, fully planned (concept, chef pack, landing page, outreach).
Impact: replaces a social agency retainer of ~£1,000–£2,000/month (£12k–£24k/yr), and the accuracy gate protects against a brand-damaging public error that could cost far more than it saves. The event is a full go-to-market (~£1,500–£3,000 of consultancy) built for near-zero spend — designed to bring in revenue, not just save it.
House of Culture · Laugharne

A live town trail and a real history archive

The Laugharne trail app is live to the public with three verified stops — and it won't publish anything it can't prove.
~1,500 sourced, verified facts behind it, at ~75% verified, with a dozen local myths debunked this week.
Impact: that archive represents hundreds of hours of professional historian research — £10k–£30k of work — plus an app build we'd otherwise pay an agency £5k–£15k for. Built and maintained by the research engine.
Sancler Property

Real photography that beats CGI

Captured the shot we'd waited weeks for — a full-tide golden-hour hero of Clôs Hugdon, plus the first real house-portrait imagery — and organised a reusable media library.
A de-risked plan to own the whole Sancler website, shedding unused, insecure WordPress pages, with instant rollback.
Impact: ~£500–£1,000 drone-photography day plus a site-migration scope an agency would bill £1k–£3k for — ahead of the same hosting/maintenance savings we just banked on Island House.
New · Software Studio

Building tools we can't buy

Stood up an in-house software studio. First product: a video editor that turns our raw footage into on-brand, on-trend social videos automatically.
Impact: aimed at replacing per-seat editing and social SaaS and, more importantly, the editor/agency time to produce social video — the same “own it, don't rent it” saving applied to content production.

The bottom line

Every business is being moved off rented, fragile platforms onto our own stack, and the tools we're building compound — each one replaces a monthly bill and a chunk of professional time, then keeps delivering week after week at near-zero cost. The savings don't happen once; they repeat.

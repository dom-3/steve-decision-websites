Deep past & the medieval town

c.60,000 years ago

Coygan Cave — the deep root

Neanderthal hand-axes a few miles up the road: the deep-time floor beneath the whole Laugharne story.

from 1116

Laugharne Castle (originally Abercorran)

The castle above the town — the anchor of the medieval borough that Island House's plot sits just outside, on the tidal inlet.

1223

First attested name of the parish

‘Sancti Michaelis de Talachar’ — the earliest recorded form, from the national historic place-names record.

The house begins

14th century

The White family — the deep root of the house

The Whites held this land from the 1300s and may have been the original builders of Island House — the earliest owners in the spine.

Owner: the White family
1437

The roof timbers of Island House

Dendrochronology dates the roof timbers to 1437 — hard proof of a high-status medieval house beside the first medieval quay ever excavated in Wales (imported pottery from Bristol, France and Spain).

1307

The lordship of Laugharne (de Brian)

The de Brian lords hold the lordship — the medieval frame the house sat within (Inquisition Post Mortem, 1307).

Tudor grandees & the Civil War

15th–16th c

The Prydderchs of Hawksbrook

Among the leading families holding the house — twice High Sheriff of the county.

Owner: Prydderch family
1575 · 1587–92

Sir John Perrot, Lord of Laugharne

Elizabeth I grants him Laugharne Castle in 1575; between 1587 and 1592 he converts the medieval fortress into a Tudor mansion. Lord Deputy of Ireland, reputedly Henry VIII's illegitimate son, who died a prisoner in the Tower — the grand neighbour to Island House in its Elizabethan heyday.

1644

The Civil War reaches the door

The town is taken; Island House sits at the heart of one of the war's most dramatic local endings — three condemned commanders who drew lots for their lives. (It was Rowland Laugharne, 1644 — not, as legend held, Cromwell.)

The Georgian merchants

18th century

The Skyrme marine-merchants

Georgian marine-supply merchants whose own trading sloop, the William Skyrme, appears in Lloyd's Register — the living link from the medieval quay to a working Georgian business.

Owner: the Skyrme family
1712–1799

Josiah Tucker of Laugharne

Born in the town — became Dean of Gloucester and a nationally important economist who argued for American independence.

1729 · 1817 · 1838

The Hughes of Laugharne

The Hughes family hold the house across the Georgian and Victorian years — three wills evidence the line (1729, 1817, 1838).

Owner: the Hughes family

Victorian & twentieth century

c.1890–1900

Percy Hughes-Garbett, resident

Confirmed at Island House through the local press (Welshman & Carmarthen Journal, 24 Jan 1890), succeeded by R. W. Pinney.

Resident: Hughes-Garbett
1900

Sold at the Brown's Hotel auction

The August 1900 estate auction — Island House is bought by Captain Thomas Jones of Llanelly.

Owner: Capt. Thomas Jones
c.1905

Thomas Titley, with tenants Schneider & Congreve

Titley as owner; Schneider and Congreve as tenants of the house.

Owner: Thomas Titley
1912–1943

John Congreve of Island House

Lieutenant, RNVR — killed in the loss of HMS Tynedale off Algeria, 1943, and named on Laugharne's Second World War roll of honour on the town war memorial.

1943–1970

Caleb Rees & Dr Laura Powell

Caleb Rees, Deputy Chief Inspector of Schools for Wales; his wife Dr Laura Powell, a pioneering woman doctor. Great-grandson of the celebrated Victorian preacher Caleb Morris.

Residents: the Rees household
1964–2020

The Walters occupancy

J. D. D. Walters — the modern chapter, through to 2020.

Owner: the Walters family

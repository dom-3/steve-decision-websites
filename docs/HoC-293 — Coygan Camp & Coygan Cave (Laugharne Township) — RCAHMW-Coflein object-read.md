## What the Coflein records establish (object-read)

**Coygan Camp — NPRN 107373** (Grid SN2842009140; Type: *Defended Enclosure*; Period: *Roman, Iron Age*; compiled J. Wiles 11.01.05, after Wainwright 1967 *Coygan Camp*):

- A **tapering oval enclosure, ~140m north–south by 60m**, set on a **steep-sided promontory** that was **surrounded on all sides except the north by former marshland** — a naturally defended spur.
- Defined by **stone-revetted ramparts (or walling)**, with an **additional rampart and ditch on the north**, the one landward-open side.
- **Excavations of 1963–4** produced **late 1st–4th-century Romano-British material and structures**, including — strikingly — **"the stock of a minting operation,"** together with **some early medieval material**. The site therefore carries an Iron Age enclosure re-used and built up through the whole Roman period, with an after-life into the early medieval centuries.
- **"Greatly, if not terminally, prejudiced by quarrying"** (cross-referenced to the quarry, NPRN 114659). The monument the record describes is substantially gone.
- Noted as **possibly associated with the Cave (NPRN 103399).**

**Coygan Cave — NPRN 103399** (Grid SN2848009130; Type: *cave / OGOF*; Period: *Palaeolithic*; compiled J. Wiles 11.01.05, with a fuller note by David Leighton, RCAHMW, 26 October 2007):

- A **limestone cave** beside the camp, **explored on occasion through the 19th–20th centuries** and, like the camp, **now destroyed by quarrying** (NPRN 114659).
- Excavation produced evidence of human and animal occupation from the **last (Devensian) Ice Age**. The **human presence dates from the Middle Palaeolithic**: finds were few but included **two hand axes of Mousterian type — associated with Neanderthals** — with this occupation **dated to c.64,000–38,000 years ago, most likely before 50,000 years ago.** Upper Palaeolithic and Postglacial use may also be represented.
- It yielded an extensive, characteristic **"hyaena den" fauna** — **woolly rhinoceros, horse and bison**. The Mousterian layer was stratified close to the base of an accumulation that possibly extended until **c.24,000 years ago.**
- Recorded as the **"possible site of a Roman patera hoard (see Ward 1976)"** — the record's own hedge, not a settled find.

Together the two records give Laugharne Township a continuous deep-past spine: a Neanderthal-used cave at the earliest horizon, and beside it a promontory enclosure occupied through the Iron Age and the entire Roman period and into the early medieval — **the town's oldest human traces, and its only Roman-period archaeology.**

### The finding, five lines

1. **Coran Bridge (Cadw 9665)** is a Grade II listed structure, Building Class **Transport**, listed **25 September 1986**, standing **"at the south end of the Cors"** (the marsh below the town), at OS grid **SN300108**.
2. It is a **small early-C19 rubble single-track bridge over the River Coran**, dated by Cadw only as "early C19" and anchored to its appearance on the **1834 survey map** — no single-year date is asserted.
3. Its fabric is **lightly cambered, with paired segmental arches with voussoirs and a modern base to the centre pier** (the "modern base" recording a later repair or rebuild of the central support), and **parapets flanking the roadway terminating at the end of the abutment**.
4. Cadw records the crossing as **"Coran"** and the watercourse as the **"River Coran"** (single r), while the town's antiquarian name **"Abercorran"** and the King Street bookshop **"Corran Books"** (Cadw 9639) use the double-r form — variant spellings of one river-name, a live instance of the standing NAME-VARIANT caution.
5. The received tradition that Laugharne's older name **Abercorran** means "mouth (*aber*) of the Corran/Coran" is **held as Tradition, not Verified**: the register's own reads already complicate it (Doc 206 — the earliest *Brut y Tywysogion* form is "Aber Cavwy," with Lloyd placing the castle "perhaps at Abercywyn"; Doc 232 — "Abercorran" first attested at 1189), and **the bridge does not evidence the etymology.**

### Reading notes and traps handled

- **No mirror conflation.** Unlike the two-listing hazard caught at HoC-299, Coran Bridge is a single, self-contained Cadw record (9665) with no adjacent structure sharing its entry, so there is no risk of collapsing two objects into one. The description was read whole.
- **The date is anchored, not invented.** Cadw gives only "early C19," with the 1834 survey map as the *terminus ante quem* — the bridge was present by 1834. No spurious build year is asserted; the "early C19, present by the 1834 survey" wording is carried into the facts verbatim in effect.
- **"Modern base to centre pier" is a repair signal, not original fabric.** It is logged as evidence of a later intervention to the central support, distinct from the early-C19 arches and parapets.
- **The Abercorran etymology is a signpost, not a fact carried by this bridge.** The "aber + Corran" reading is widely repeated, but the register has already refined it (Docs 206, 232). It is logged only as Tradition, cross-referenced to those docs, and explicitly **not** promoted on the strength of a bridge that merely shares the river's name. No attempt was made to reconcile the "Coran"/"Corran" spellings by assumption.
- **No Dylan or Globe hook was manufactured** and none exists in this record. The bridge is town fabric read on its own terms, per the Doc-140 register — a Cors-edge river crossing, a Stream-F/H object in its own right, with no literary association attached.

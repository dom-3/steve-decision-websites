## Five-line finding

1. **A matched pair, one description.** As with Gwalia/Manchester House (Doc 218), Cadw writes a **single shared exterior description** covering both units: "Pair of C18/early C19 [2]-storey, 2-window whitewashed rubble frontage" — so 9660 and 9661 are one architectural composition, listed together.
2. **Date and grade.** Both **Grade II**, listed **25 September 1986** (the same 1986 Laugharne sweep that listed Gwalia/Manchester House and much of the town centre), **Building Class Domestic**. Style is late-Georgian (C18/early C19); no documentary build year is given.
3. **Listed for group value.** The stated Reason for Listing is **group value** — the pair is protected for its townscape contribution beside the Grade II\* Town Hall and the Globe, not for individual architectural distinction. A documentable statement about the Globe/Town Hall setting.
4. **The fabric.** Two-storey, two-window whitewashed rubble frontages; **No.2** has slightly higher eaves, a steeper slate roof and a **4-pane shop window**; brick and cement-rendered chimney stacks; various small-pane sash windows; a 6-panel door to the left and a half-glazed door to the right, the latter **under a brick segmental arch**. The shop window records the pair's commercial use on the market frontage.
5. **No.1 excluded.** Cadw records that **No.1 Market Street continues to the right but "is no longer of special interest"** — the historic run was Nos 1–3, but only Nos 2 and 3 retain listable interest. No occupant is named on either object, and no exact build year is documented.

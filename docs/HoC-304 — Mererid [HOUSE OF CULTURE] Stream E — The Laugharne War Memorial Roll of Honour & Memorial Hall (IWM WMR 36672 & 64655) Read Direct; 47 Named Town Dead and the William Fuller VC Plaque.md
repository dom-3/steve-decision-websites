## One-line finding

Laugharne's war dead are recorded on two black slate tablets — 35 of the First World War, 12 of the Second — now standing at the **Memorial Hall, Clifton Street**, with a **separate plaque to the town's Victoria Cross holder, William Charles Fuller VC**, set at their base; all three memorials read direct from the IWM War Memorials Register, and the full roll reconciled name-by-name against the Steven John (2004) transcription.

## The memorial and the hall

The memorial proper is **two black (slate) tablets with white lettering, set on a rough-edged white stone** — one tablet for each war — standing in a paved, brick-bordered enclosure with a bench to each side and a modern wooden cross, at the **Memorial Hall, Clifton Street, Laugharne, SA33 4QG** (OS grid **SN 30188 10978**). Inscriptions, verbatim from WMR‑36672:

> 1914‑1918 / ROLL OF HONOUR / LAUGHARNE / (Names) / "NOW ARE THEY CROWNED AND RECIEVE PALMS"
> 1939‑45 / ROLL OF HONOUR / LAUGHARNE / (Names) / "WE WILL REMEMBER THEM"

("RECIEVE" is the spelling cut on the stone.)

The **Memorial Hall itself is a war memorial** (WMR‑64655): "a large modern hall with a stone tablet with dedicatory inscription set upon a triangular-headed frame with columns at either side. A local emblem is set between the two sets of dates." Its inscription:

> LEST WE FORGET / 1914 1918 / 1939 1945 / THIS HALL IS ERECTED TO THE IMMORTAL MEMORY OF OUR FALLEN HEROES

**Data note:** the two IWM records disagree on the address — WMR‑64655 gives "at St Martins Church, Off King St" (SA33 4QQ) while WMR‑36672 gives "Memorial Hall, Clifton Street" (SA33 4QG) — but both carry the *same* grid reference (SN 30188 10978). Not resolved here; treat "Memorial Hall, Clifton Street" as the working location. The hall is modern and **not Cadw-listed**.

## The counts and the reconciliation

IWM WMR‑36672 records **35 First World War dead and 12 Second World War dead — 47 named in all**, each with surname, rank, regiment and forename.

The Steven John (2004) compilation lists **36 WW1 + 14 WW2** — three more than the tablets — and expressly flags the non-corresponding names, because it "is a compilation between the Roll of Honour book and the War Memorial." The three flagged **"Not On Memorial"** are:

- **Thomas Rees Waters** (WW1)
- **Bridgeman Rochford Mordaunt‑Smith** (WW2)
- **Thomas Brian Owen** (WW2)

Remove those three and the compilation reconciles **exactly** to the 35 + 12 on the tablets. So the memorial and the "Roll of Honour book" are two distinct records that mostly, but not perfectly, overlap. *(Verified.)*

## William Charles Fuller VC — the plaque at the base

At the base of the memorial stands **a separate stone plaque to William Fuller** (WMR‑36672: "a separate stone plaque to William Fuller — see separate entry"). Fuller **survived** the war, so he is commemorated apart from the two Rolls of Honour — the town's honouring of its Victoria Cross man.

From the **London Gazette citation of 23 November 1914**, read verbatim:

> For conspicuous gallantry on 14th September 1914 near Chivy on the Aisne, by advancing about 100 yards to pick up Captain Haggard, who was mortally wounded, and carrying him back to cover under very heavy rifle and machine gun fire.

Awarded to **No. 7753 Lance-Corporal William Charles Fuller, 2nd Battalion, The Welch Regiment**; invested by King George V at Buckingham Palace on **13 January 1915**; he later rose to **Sergeant**. He died aged 90 on **29 December 1974** and is buried in **Oystermouth Cemetery, The Mumbles** (his grave unmarked until a headstone was erected c.2005). *(VC facts: Verified-strong.)*

His birth on **13 March 1884 at Laugharne**, and the frequently-repeated claim that he was **the first Welsh-born VC of the First World War**, rest on secondary biographies (not read at primary object level this pass) — **tier Referenced-pending**. The plaque's existence at the Laugharne memorial is itself Verified from the IWM object.

## Guardrails set this pass

- **Near-collision — "Brynmor John".** The senior WW2 name, **Commander Henry Brynmor John, MBE, RN (d. 26 Aug 1946)**, is **not** the Labour politician **Brynmor John MP (1934–1988, Pontypridd)** — a different, later man. Logged as a data-integrity fact.
- **"John" is the commonest Laugharne surname** — several men of that name sit on both tablets (two John James's in the WW1 Welsh Regiment alone). Attach forename and service number before identifying any "John."
- **Do not upgrade the birthplace or "first Welsh VC" claim** for Fuller without a primary object.
- **The two IWM address records disagree**; do not assert a single street until resolved.

It recorded that the 1910 chancel/rood screen to **Edward Wienholt and his wife Ellen Williams Wienholt** was given by their **three sons and three daughters**, the sons being *Arnold Wienholt, Edward Arthur Wienholt and Wm. That lead is the target of this pass. It matters because "Arnold Wienholt" is a name that repeats in this family, and because the 1877–1940 man is a figure of independent note (soldier, explorer, author, Australian politician) — exactly the shape of claim that must be nailed to an object rather than assumed.

## The object

Read at object level: the **Australian Dictionary of Biography**, D. B. Waterson, *"Wienholt, Arnold (1826–1895)"*, ADB Volume 6 (Melbourne University Press, 1976; online 2006), at `adb.anu.edu.au/biography/wienholt-arnold-4849`. This is a **shared entry** covering the two pastoralist brothers **Arnold Wienholt (1826–1895)** and **Edward Wienholt (1833–1904)**. The ADB is an authoritative national biographical dictionary — the Australian counterpart of the DWB/ODNB, and treated here as a T2 biographical-dictionary source read directly, not a signpost.

The entry states, in its own words, that Arnold (1826) and Edward (1833) "were sons of John Birkett Wienholt (1775–1852), merchant, and his second wife Sarah, née Hill," both born "at Laugharne, Carmarthenshire, Wales"; that two further brothers, Daniel (1822–1865) and Arthur (1835–1892), "were also prominent Queensland pioneers"; and that the family, "originally Winholdt, originated at Wiemsdorf, Oldenburg, Germany, in the sixteenth century." Of Edward it records his marriage "on 14 December 1874 to Ellen (1856–1898), daughter of Daniel Williams, railway contractor and entrepreneur," and that "they had three sons and three daughters, including Arnold (1877–1940)."

## The finding

**1. Identification confirmed.** The screen donor "Arnold Wienholt" of Doc 296 is **Arnold Wienholt (1877–1940)**. The two objects coincide on both parents and the child: Doc 296's screen is to *Edward & Ellen Wienholt*, given by their sons including *Arnold*; the ADB names *Edward & Ellen's* children as "three sons and three daughters, including Arnold (1877–1940)." (F300-1, Verified.)

**2. The two-Arnold near-collision, resolved.** There are **two** Arnold Wienholts and they must never be conflated. The **uncle**, Arnold Wienholt (1826–1895), was a Darling Downs studmaster (Maryvale, Gladfield), briefly MLA for Warwick (1863–67), who retired to Locarno and **died unmarried** on 16 January 1895. The **nephew**, Arnold Wienholt (1877–1940), is Edward's son. Because the screen donors are Edward & Ellen's *own children*, the donor is necessarily the nephew — so Doc 296's tentative lead ("very plausibly the Anglo-Australian soldier … 1877–1940") is now adopted, and the uncle's biography is fenced off from the memorial. (F300-2, Verified.)

**3. The Laugharne merchant root, fixed.** The Wienholts of Laugharne descend from **John Birkett Wienholt (1775–1852), merchant**, whose sons Daniel (1822–65), Arnold (1826–95), Edward (1833–1904) and Arthur (1835–92) were all born at Laugharne before emigrating to build one of colonial Queensland's largest pastoral empires. The surname was originally "Winholdt," of Wiemsdorf, Oldenburg, Germany, in the sixteenth century. This supplies the merchant-family context behind both the Queensland dynasty and the St Martin's memorials. (F300-3, Verified.)

**4. Honest boundary — Edward's limb is the Queensland branch, not Island House.** Edward Wienholt (1833–1904) emigrated to Queensland in 1853, acquired Fassifern, Jondaryan and Goomburra with William Kent, formed the Wienholt Pastoral Estates Co. (1889), sat as MLA for Western Downs (1870–73) and Darling Downs (1873–75), and retired to Rocklands, Ross-on-Wye, Herefordshire, in 1880, dying in Melbourne on 14 January 1904. He was **not himself an Island House resident**. Doc 296's newspaper line that the Weinholts "occupied Laugharne Castle and Island House" pertains to the *wider Laugharne family* — chiefly Frederick Wienholt of Island House (Doc 226) — not to Edward personally. (F300-4, Verified.)

**5. Arnold (1877–1940) himself has no Island House residence.** His connection to the house is **familial** (he is a member of the Laugharne Wienholt merchant family) and **commemorative** (he co-gave his parents' St Martin's screen). His own career — Wixenford and Eton; the Queensland Imperial Bushmen in the Boer War; the British South Africa Police and East African Mounted Rifles in the First World War; the Military Cross and DSO; MLA for Fassifern (1909–13, 1930–35); MHR for Moreton (1919–22); killed in action in Abyssinia on 10 September 1940 and commemorated on the Khartoum Memorial; author of *The Story of a Lion Hunt* (1922) and other books — is here **reported from tertiary signposts** (Wikipedia, the Queensland Parliament former-members list, the Commonwealth War Graves Commission), not object-read from a biographical dictionary this pass. His identity, dates and parentage are Verified from the ADB; the career detail is held **Referenced-pending** until read in his own biographical-dictionary entry (none located this pass). (F300-5, Referenced-pending.)

**6. Open onward lead (not adopted).** The ADB names only Daniel, Arnold, Edward and Arthur as sons of John Birkett Wienholt. **Frederick Wienholt of Island House** (occupant 1863–1881; Doc 226) is not among them; his exact kinship to this line remains **UNSOURCED** — a further sibling, half-sibling, or adjacent-generation relation. ## Method notes and guardrails

- **Object over snippet.** The identification rests on the ADB text itself, read at object level, not on the search-result paraphrase that first surfaced it.

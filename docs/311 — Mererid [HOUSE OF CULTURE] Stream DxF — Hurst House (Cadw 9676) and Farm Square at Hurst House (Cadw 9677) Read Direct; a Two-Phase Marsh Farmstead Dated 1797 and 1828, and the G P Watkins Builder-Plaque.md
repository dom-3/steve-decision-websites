### Hurst House — Cadw LB 9676

- Grade II; listed **25 September 1986**; Building Class **"Commercial"**; reason for listing: **Group value**. BLB 300009676; Wikidata Q29488433; OS grid SN296086.
- Location: "Situated in the marshes to S of Laugharne near the MOD area. Set back from road in enclosed small garden to N of the former farmyard."
- **History (verbatim): "Dated 1797."**
- Exterior: a Georgian 3-storey, 3-window rendered front, slightly advanced from flanking 2-storey lean-tos, with outbuildings forward to left and (modern) right; slate roof; cornice with diagonally set bricks; rubble and chimney stacks; casement windows to the 2nd floor, sashes below and to the lean-to; an arched entrance and a scribed panelled door below a plaque. A rendered 2-storey, 5-window rear with small-pane glazed sashes and a lower whitewashed rubble lean-to on the south end.
- **The builder-plaque (verbatim): "This House and offices were erected by G P Watkins Esq. Anno Domini 1797."**

### Farm Square at Hurst House — Cadw LB 9677

- Grade II; listed **25 September 1986**; Building Class **"Agriculture and Subsistence"**; reason for listing: **Group value**. BLB 300009677; Wikidata Q29488434; OS grid SN296086.
- Location: "To S of and adjoining Hurst House in the marshes to S of Laugharne."
- **History (verbatim): "Dated 1828; model farm plan."**
- Exterior: a large square grassed former farmyard reached through a segmental voussoired arch in a whitewashed rubble wall lining the road (forming the E range, with a low lean-to to the inner side); a similar opening with a **dated keystone** to the former **coach house** on the S end; a **dovecote** to a gable end reached by outside rubble steps to the left; **loading platforms** to the right. Two-storey whitewashed rubble and slate-roofed S and W ranges; part-whitewashed outer wall to the S range; various square-headed openings to the upper floor and segmental voussoir openings to the ground floor. Later alterations include modern conversion from farm usage. Further similar outbuildings detached to the N.

## Finding — a documented two-phase marsh farmstead

The two listings together record a farmstead built out in **two dated phases, each fixed by its own inscription rather than by style alone**:

1. **1797** — the dwelling "and offices," a Georgian gentleman-farmer's house, erected by **G P Watkins Esq.** and named on a door-plaque.
2. **1828** — the planned **"model farm"** square, a 31-years-later agricultural-improvement layout with a formal entrance arch, coach house (dated keystone), dovecote and loading platforms, on the reclaimed East Marsh south of the town.

This is a rare thing to have from a listing: a dwelling and its farm range each carrying its own date, thirty years apart, on the same holding. The 1828 "model farm plan" phrase places the farmyard squarely in the 1820s agricultural-improvement movement, and the pairing gives the reclaimed Laugharne marshes (the "Menetors" material of Doc 166; the neighbouring East House Farm of Doc 152) a datable improved-farmstead exemplar.

## Named builder — G P Watkins Esq. (held partly pending)

The plaque names the builder **"G P Watkins Esq."** (1797). The inscription itself is Verified from the object. The man is otherwise unidentified from the listing, and — importantly — **the tempting link to the Watkins family of Broadway (Doc 183, "Pennoyre Watkins settled as the Broadway Watkins") is NOT asserted.** Surname coincidence in a small township is not evidence; the connection is logged **Referenced-pending** for a later directory / probate / estate-papers pass. The forename initials "G P" are left unexpanded, as the object leaves them.

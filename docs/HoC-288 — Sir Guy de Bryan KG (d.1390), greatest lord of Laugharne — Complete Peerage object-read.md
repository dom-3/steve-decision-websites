## What the object establishes (five findings)

**1. Birth and the 1349 succession — the "30 vs 40" crux, settled from the primary IPM (Verified).**
Guy de Bryan (d.1390) *succeeded his father in 1349.* The Complete Peerage's main text (vol. 2 p.361) gave his age at succession as 40, and vol. 14 "corrected" it — but his **father's own inquisition post mortem, dated the Saturday after the Translation of St Thomas the Martyr [11 July] 1349, gives his age as "30 years and more"** (*Calendar of Inquisitions post mortem*, vol. 9, no. 333, p. 282). That restores a birth of **c.1318–19**, corroborating from the primary IPM the register's existing "b. c.1319" fact. His father was **Sir Guy de Bryan of Walwyn's Castle (co. Pembroke) and Tor Bryan (Devon)** — the Pembrokeshire/Devon estate base of the family that also held the marcher lordship of Laugharne.

**2. The heir-apparent predeceased him — the pivot (Verified).**
Guy's only son and heir-apparent, **Sir Guy de Bryan "the younger," died in his father's lifetime (v.p.) in 1386** — will dated 7 July 1383, **proved at Rampisham, Dorset, on 30 March 1386**; IPM 9 Ric. II (1385/6). He married **Alice, daughter and heir of Sir Robert de Bures of Bures St Mary, Suffolk** (living 20 May 1409; executrix to her father-in-law 27 January 1393/4). This predecease is the hinge of the whole descent.

**3. The line failed to coheiresses; the barony fell into abeyance (Verified).**
Because the son died first, the heirs of Guy de Bryan (d.1390) were **his granddaughters** (the younger Guy's daughters). The **Barony of Bryan — created by writ of summons to Parliament 24 Edw. III (1350)** — therefore passed to coheirs and fell into abeyance. The Complete Peerage's long note h traces the later claims: by an enrolment of **16 December 1488** (*Materials illustrative of the Reign of Henry VII*, vol. ii, pp. 380–83), the *"right heir to the said Sir Guy de Brien"* is named as **Dame Avice, Countess of Wiltshire**, and the Earl of Northumberland is reckoned *"heir general to the said Sir Guy de Brian."* (The note also demolishes, at length, the Percy/Northumberland pretension to the *Barony of Bryan* created by the 1350 writ — a caution logged but not central here.)

**4. This is the tenurial mechanism behind Cadw's "long decline" (Referenced-pending synthesis).**
The failure of the direct de Brian male line in 1390, its inheritance splitting among coheiress granddaughters, is the concrete reason Laugharne had no resident de Brian lord thereafter — the *tenurial* fact underneath Cadw SAM 3078's statement that after 1390 Laugharne entered *"a long period of decline"* lasting until the **1575 Perrot grant** (HoC-270). The two components are each Verified (coheirs = CP; decline-to-1575 = Cadw); the causal link is tiered **Referenced-pending** because the exact post-1390 manorial descent *of Laugharne specifically* is not yet read at object level.

**5. Why Laugharne's absence from the 1390 IPM is not a disproof (Referenced-pending caution).**
The existing Verified fact — that Laugharne is not listed in Guy's 1390 CIPM — is **consistent with Laugharne being a Welsh marcher lordship lying outside the English shire escheatry.** Marcher lords held with quasi-regalian authority, and their Welsh lordships were not routinely returned in the county inquisitions post mortem, just as (register-Verified, entity rechUv4thsUiBnSkA) the de Brian **borough charter was seigneurial and was never enrolled on the royal rolls** because marcher lords granted by their own authority. So the omission does **not** by itself prove he had alienated Laugharne before death. Tiered Referenced-pending as an interpretive caution; it does not overturn the Verified absence-fact, it explains why absence is not decisive.

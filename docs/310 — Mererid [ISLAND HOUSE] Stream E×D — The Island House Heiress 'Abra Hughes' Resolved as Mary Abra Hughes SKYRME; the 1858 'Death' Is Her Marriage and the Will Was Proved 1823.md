## 1. The question

Doc 177 registered a puzzle (F177-3): a source styled a woman **"Abra Hughes, only daughter and heiress of William Skyrme, Esq., of Laugharne."** If her surname were *Hughes*, how could she be the daughter and heiress of a man named *Skyrme*? Doc 177 also produced an entity **"William Skyrme, Esq., of Laugharne (d. by Aug 1858)"**, which pinned the death — and therefore the will — to roughly 1830–1858. The thread asked to find that will.

The premise is faulty on both counts. There is no surname problem, and there is no 1858 death.

## 2. What the objects say

Three independent objects were read at object level this pass.

**(a) The Cadw statutory listing — a physical plaque.** The Cadw listed-building record for the *Garage to Island House / boundary wall* (Cadw ID **9673**, Grade II, listed 25 September 1986; read via the British Listed Buildings mirror of the statutory text) describes the boundary-wall section and records, on its inner face, a plaque reading verbatim:

> **"This wall was rebuilt by Maryabra Hughes Skyrme AD 1837"**

This is hard fabric, independent of any genealogy. It fixes both the **name form** ("Maryabra Hughes Skyrme" — surname *Skyrme*) and the fact that she was in possession of and improving the property in **1837**. Island House itself is Grade II\* (Cadw 9671); the boundary wall is 9672; the garage 9673.

**(b) The Island House owner-history.** Island House Restoration Ltd's compiled owner-by-owner history (*"The Owners and People of Island House | 1778–2020"*, islandhouse.wales), drawing on census, the National Probate Calendar, the 1834 Corporation and 1842 tithe surveys and local newspapers, names her consistently as **Mary Abra Hughes Skyrme**, only daughter and sole heiress of **William Skyrme III (1778–1823)** and **Mary Lewis of Henllan**. It records her marriage to **Frederick Wienholt** of London in **1858** (she was then 45); Frederick as head of the Island House household in the 1881 census, dying at Laugharne 14 April 1881; and her own death, childless, in **1885**, the estate passing to **Percy Llewelyn (Hughes-)Garbett**.

**(c) The Skyrme One-Name Study.** David J Skyrme, *"The Sloop 'William Skyrme'"* (*Dyfed Family History Journal* Vol 13 No 7, pp 12–15, Dec 2019; skyrme.info), independently confirms the paternal line: **William Skyrme** (b. Llawhaden c.1746, d. Laugharne 1788) and wife **Jane**, whose five children — all born in Laugharne, and living at **Island House** — included the eldest son **William (1778–1823)**. The family were descendants of the wealthy Llawhaden Skyrmes; William's father was Portreeve of Laugharne in 1775, and William himself Portreeve in 1801–2 and 1817. William married **Mary Lewis** on 17 May 1811; the sloop was built at Laugharne that same year.

## 3. Resolution

**"Abra Hughes" are forenames, not a surname.** Her full style was **Mary Abra Hughes Skyrme** — surname **Skyrme** throughout. "Only daughter and heiress of William Skyrme" carrying the byname "Abra Hughes" is therefore no contradiction at all. (Fact `recjKLlsV3bYxzFyu`, **Verified** — the plaque, the owner-history and the One-Name Study agree.)

**The "Hughes" element is paternal.** Her grandmother was **Jane Hughes**, wife of William Skyrme II (documented entity *"William Skyrme II & Jane Hughes"*). Carrying a grandmother's maiden surname as a Christian name was standard gentry practice, and is the most economical explanation for the "Hughes" in her name. Recorded as **Referenced-pending** (Fact `recP8kkkyoXwPJrjl`): Jane Hughes is documented; the naming inference is interpretive, not stated in the sources. The forename **"Abra"** itself remains unexplained and is left to its own thread (`recITGZ4zRNlIJ7F9`).

**There was no 1858 death.** William Skyrme III died in **1823** — his PCC will was **proved 27 August 1823, TNA PROB 11/1674/373** (already located and logged under Doc 179 / T-Skyrme-will, entity `recnvZDMfKZ8FMiqn`). The "1858" that generated the Doc 177 entity *"William Skyrme, Esq., of Laugharne (d. by Aug 1858)"* is his **daughter's 1858 marriage** to Frederick Wienholt, in which she would have been styled *"only daughter and heiress of the late William Skyrme."* "The late William Skyrme" (d. 1823) was misread as a c.1858 death. The thread's search parameter — a will "proved c.1830–1858" — was chasing a will that had in fact been proved a generation earlier. (Fact `recOhI8xFX8i4gSlM`, **Verified**.)

So the will the thread set out to find already exists in our register (PROB 11/1674/373, 1823). No new will hunt is required to answer F177-3; the question is answered by the name form and the 1823 probate.

## 4. Occupancy spine touched (context, not new claims)

For continuity, the owner-history places the Skyrme/heiress line in the wider Island House succession: William Skyrme III (d. 1823) → his widow Mary Lewis remarries **George Bosville Wentworth Stackpoole** (1828), the couple resident at Island House per the 1841/51 census, holding land *of* the heiress → **Mary Abra Hughes Skyrme**, sole heiress, m. Frederick Wienholt 1858, d. 1885 childless → **Percy Llewelyn Hughes-Garbett** (a Lewis-of-Henllan relative, who prepended "Hughes" as a condition of inheritance) → the **1900 break-up of the Laugharne estate by auction at Brown's Hotel** (~£25,000), Island House itself bought by **Capt. Thomas Jones** for £1,000. These successor details are compiled-secondary and are **not** promoted here.

## Findings

The one-line thread framing ("Ellen Weinholt gave the rood/chancel screen and the lychgate for her sister Mary") turns out to **conflate three separate memorials**. Read apart:

**1. The rood/chancel screen — Edward and Ellen Wienholt, 1909/1910.**
The Cadw listing dates the interior "Ornate rood screen and loft of 1909 (former rood loft door blocked by commemorative tablet)." The dedication was reported in full by *The Welshman* (1 Apr 1910, p.8): on Sunday evening 27 March 1910 the Bishop of St David's "unveiled and dedicated an elegantly designed screen (erected in the entrance to the chancel) to the memory of the late Ed. Weinholt, Esq." — oak, fifteenth-century style, five bays, about 14 ft high, its central shield bearing "the crest of the Weinholt family, and the date A.D. 1910." It was **designed by Chas. E. Ponting, F.S.A., of Marlborough, and executed by Herbert Read of the St Sidwell's Art Works, Exeter.** The same report states the screen is "erected to the memory of **Edward Wienholt and his wife Ellen Williams Wienholt**." Corroborated by the *Evening Express* (28 Mar 1910): "in memory of Edward Weinholt and Ellen Williams-Weinholt, his wife." (F296-1, **Verified-strong**.)

**2. The screen's donors — the six children.**
Both papers name the donors as the **three sons and three daughters** of Edward and Ellen: *daughters* Muriel Gwendoline Hulse, Brenda, and Magdalene Mary Browne-Clayton; *sons* Arnold Wienholt, Edward Arthur Wienholt, and Wm. Humphrey Meyrick Wienholt. "Hulse" and "Browne-Clayton" are married-daughter surnames. (F296-2, **Verified**.) *Lead, not adopted:* "Arnold Wienholt" is very plausibly the Anglo-Australian soldier, explorer and politician Arnold Wienholt (1877–1940) of the Queensland pastoralist family of Welsh origin — but that identification is **not made at object level** this pass and is recorded only as an onward lead.

**3. The lych-gate — Helen Weinholt for her sister Mary Frances, 1913.**
*The Cambria Daily Leader* (31 Jul 1913, p.5, "Brevities"): "Archdeacon Williams, St. David's, [yesterday] dedicated a lych-gate at St. Martin's Church, Laugharne, which has b[ee]n erected by **Miss Helen Weinholt** in memory of her sister, **Miss Mary Frances Weinholt, who died at Reading in [18]96.**" No separately listed lych-gate exists in Laugharne Township (the full Cadw township list was checked), so this dedication report is the record of it. (F296-3, **Verified**.)

**4. The near-collision RESOLVED — Weinholt = Wienholt = the Island House family.**
The same 1913 item states directly: "**The Weinholt family were for many years residents of the town, occupying Laugharne Castle and Island House.**" This is the Tier-1 statement that ties the St Martin's Weinholt memorials to the Island House occupant **Frederick Wienholt** (1863–1881, Doc 226). The spelling is variance, not two families: *The Welshman*'s 1 Apr 1910 report prints the surname as **both** "Weinholt" **and** "Wienholt" within a single paragraph. So the thread's spelling near-collision is settled — one family. (F296-4, **Verified**.) *Guardrail:* the "Laugharne Castle" occupancy is the newspaper's own assertion; it echoes Doc 177's OCR "the Castle, Laugharne" for J. Birkett Wienholt but does **not** promote Doc 172's flagged "(of the Castle)" epithet — recorded, not upgraded.

**5. A Frederick tablet, bracketed.**
*The Welshman* pre-announcement (25 Mar 1910) said the Bishop would "unveil a memorial inside the Church to the late Mr. **Frederick** Weinholt" — the Island House man, d.1881 — at that same 27 March service. The post-event report (1 Apr) names the screen to **Edward**. Best reconciliation: the **screen** commemorates Edward & Ellen, and a separate **interior tablet** (the "commemorative tablet" the Cadw listing records blocking the former rood-loft door) commemorates Frederick. This is not settled — the tablet's own inscription was not read at object level — so the Frederick/Edward forename discrepancy is held **Referenced-pending** (F296-5).

**6. The donor-attribution corrected.**
The thread's "Ellen Weinholt … for her sister Mary" is corrected by the objects: the lych-gate donor is **Helen** Weinholt (for her sister Mary Frances); "**Ellen**" Williams-Weinholt is a distinct person — Edward's wife and a screen dedicatee — not the lych-gate donor. The screen (1910) and the lych-gate (1913) are two different memorials with different subjects. Helen/Ellen is exactly the kind of near-matching name the search traps warn about, here separated by reading each object. (F296-6, **Verified**.)

**Resulting picture (Island House family limb):** the Weinholts/Wienholts of Island House left several marks on St Martin's — Frederick's c.1873 restoration of the tower, transepts, porch and infants' school (Docs 225/226); an interior tablet to Frederick (c.1910, pending); the chancel screen to Edward & Ellen given by their six children (1910); and Helen's lych-gate for Mary Frances (1913). Frederick's exact kinship to Edward, and Helen's and Mary Frances's parentage, are not yet fixed.

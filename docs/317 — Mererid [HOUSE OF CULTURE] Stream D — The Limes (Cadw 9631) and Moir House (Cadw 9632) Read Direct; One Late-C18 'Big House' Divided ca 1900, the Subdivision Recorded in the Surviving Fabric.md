### Five-line finding

1. **The two houses are one house.** Cadw records — independently on both objects — that The Limes and Moir House were "built with" each other and "known as **Big House**", a single late-C18 Georgian house "divided up ca 1900." This is the whole point of the pair: not two neighbours but two halves of one mansion, the largest Georgian house on the Clifton Street slope.

2. **Late eighteenth century; the "1790" hedged.** Both listings date the house "Late C18", with "one roof truss said to be dated 1790." The 1790 anchor is a roof-truss tradition inside the statutory text, so it is held **Referenced-pending** and the fabric is anchored to "late eighteenth century", not the numeral — "late C18" itself is Verified.

3. **The subdivision is written into the surviving fabric.** The Limes kept the principal set-piece of Big House — the "fine open pedimented doorcase with oval paterae to bracketed entablature", 6-panel door and 5-panel petal fanlight, plus a four-storey splayed bay. Moir House kept "what was the main staircase to Big House, formerly with pyramidal lantern", and its own listing states it "formerly [had a] storeyed bay to right to balance the front **when one property with The Limes**." The ca-1900 division added Moir House's splayed ground-floor bays (iron railings, coloured glass). The architecture itself is the evidence for the story.

4. **An early listing.** Both were listed **30 November 1966** — the early Cadw sweep (the same date as Great House II* and Laugharne Castle), well before the 25 September 1986 sweep that caught most of the town's group-value frontage houses (Gwalia/Manchester House, Gainsford/Osbourne, Fen Hill/Fernhill). A 1966 listing now carried under "group value" is itself a small point about how the town's fabric was first recognised.

5. **Setting.** The pair sits across the slope at the junction with Clifton Street, reached by a ramped, iron-railed pavement above the road; Moir House is attached to The Limes on the left and, at an angle, to the already-registered **Rosetta** (Cadw 9633, Doc 309) on the right, opposite **Great House** (Cadw 9650, Grade II*). This extends the desk's slope/frontage sweep and ties directly into the Rosetta / Aylmer node.

### The object, read

**The Limes (Cadw 9631, Grade II, listed 30 Nov 1966, Building Class Domestic, group value).** "Late C18 (said to be dated 1790) Georgian 3-storey and basement 2-window rendered front built with Moir House and known as 'Big House', Divided up ca 1900." Slate roof, dentiled eaves; brick chimney stacks (later to right) with a rubble chimney breast to the left end wall; 3-light horned sash windows with a four-storey splayed bay to the left; a raised central entrance reached by parallel rubble steps, with the fine open pedimented doorcase (oval paterae to a bracketed entablature), a 6-panel door and 5-panel petal fanlight; a basement window retained to the left. A 3-storey, 2-window rubble cross range stands at the rear with irregularly spaced sash, pivot and casement windows over brick voussoirs; the rear porch has been removed. Interior: classically detailed fireplace, egg-and-dart and acanthus cornices, panelled doors.

**Moir House (Cadw 9632, Grade II, listed 30 Nov 1966, Building Class Domestic, group value).** "Late C18, one roof truss said to be dated 1790. Georgian 3-storey, 3-window rendered front with later alterations, built with The Limes and known as Big House; divided up ca 1900." Slate roof, dentiled eaves, brick chimney stacks; small-pane sash windows to the first and second floors (horns to the latter); ca-1900 splayed bays to the ground floor with iron railings to the flat roofs, advanced dentiled cornices, and some coloured glass above the sash windows; central arched entrances with a trellis porch, a recessed 6-panel door and 5-pane petal fanlight. It "formerly [had a] storeyed bay to right to balance the front when one property with The Limes." Rendered rear with pyramidal-roofed and lower gabled extensions. Interior retains "what was the main staircase to Big House, formerly with pyramidal lantern", with classical cornices and fireplaces.

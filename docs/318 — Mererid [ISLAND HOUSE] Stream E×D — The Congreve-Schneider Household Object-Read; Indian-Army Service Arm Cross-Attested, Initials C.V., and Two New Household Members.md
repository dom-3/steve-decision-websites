## Findings (object-level)

**1. Service arm — INDIAN ARMY (cross-attested).** The occupier was an Indian-Army officer. Two *independent* local-history objects agree:
- islandhouse.wales: "Major Schneider of the Indian Army …"
- laugharnelines.wales: "He was a friend from Indian Army days of Colonel Bolton of Elm House …"

This answers Doc 316's open "regiment" question at the **service-arm** level. It is consistent with the father's career (Bombay Army). The **specific regiment** is still not recovered. Tier: **Verified** in the register's cross-attested-secondary sense (two independent local histories), *not* court-grade / Verified-strong. (F318-1)

**2. Initials at object level, and the forename tension recorded precisely.** islandhouse.wales heads the section "**Major Claude Vyvian Congreve (Schneider)**" and, in the body, "**Major C.V Schneider** was the son of Brigadier General John William Schneider" — so the initials **C.V.** are on the object, matching "Claude Vyvian". But the *same page* then quotes the National Probate Calendar 1903 verbatim:

> "SCHNEIDER sir John William … General in H.M. Bombay Army died 27 May 1903 Probate London 23rd June to Dame Amelia Euphemia Schneider widow and **Stewart Melville Congreve Schneider major in H.M. Army** …"

So on one object we have two forenames for Gen. Schneider's major son — "Claude Vyvian" (the compilers' heading) and "Stewart Melville Congreve Schneider" (the 1903 probate). Whether these are **the same major or two brothers** is itself unresolved. The forename is therefore **held Referenced-pending**; the **1923 probate** (the major died at Island House 17 Feb 1923) remains the tie-break. This advances F316-1 from a Wikipedia signpost to an object-level read of the same tension — but does not close it. (F318-2)

**3. Owner–occupier tie, object-confirmed.** islandhouse.wales states Capt. Thomas Jones "then sold Island House to Mr Thomas Titley who was the father of the wife of Major Congreve (Schneider) Mary Hudson Congreve." This promotes Doc 314's *inferred* "Titley = father-in-law" tie (owner F225-3 ↔ occupier F225-5) to an object-level statement. Tier: **Verified** (secondary owner-history). (F318-6)

**4. Prior residence.** Before Island House the major "had previously lived in Fullerton" (laugharnelines.wales) — a lead, unlocated (several places bear that name), not firmed. Referenced-pending. (F318-3)

**5. New household member — son John, born in the house.** "Major Congreve's son John, born in Island House in 1912, died in the Battle of Algeria in 1942 aged 31" (laugharnelines.wales). A named household member with a **direct in-house connection** (born there). The phrase "Battle of Algeria" is a local-history imprecision — there was no engagement of that exact name; it is probably the November 1942 North-Africa landings / Operation Torch. The exact action is **not asserted**; the year (1942) and age (31) are recorded as given. New entity + F318-4.

**6. New family member in the town — the major's mother.** "The mother of Major Schneider Dame Amelia Euphemia Schneider was listed in the newspaper as passing away (16/03/1915) in Laugharne … widow of Sir John Schneider, K.C.B., and daughter of the late Colonel Chas. James Colley Davidson, Bengal Engineers … gross value £867" (islandhouse.wales, quoting a newspaper notice). This places the **wider Schneider family in Laugharne itself** during the tenancy, not only the house. Primary quoted by secondary — Referenced-pending. New entity + F318-5.

**Colour, not asserted as fact:** the household was artistic — the major played banjo and sang, his wife played piano, both were skilled watercolourists, and "regular rehearsals and soirees took place well into the 1930s" (laugharnelines.wales). Recorded here as texture; not tiered.

## 1. The question

Kelly's *Directory of Monmouthshire & South Wales*, 1895, lists among Laugharne's private residents: **"Dowden, Col., Island house, Wogan street."** The House of Culture desk read that directory (HoC-273) and flagged the entry as an Island House occupancy-spine matter for this desk. The task: fix Colonel Dowden's identity, rank, dates and tenure at Island House against the open, readable record — without inventing anything the object does not earn.

## 2. What the objects say

Three independent contemporary sources, read at object level, place a **Colonel Dowden at Island House, Laugharne, across 1895–1896**:

1. **Kelly's Directory, 1895** — private-residents list: *"Dowden, Col., Island house, Wogan street."* (Island House stands on Wogan Street; the address is internally consistent.) Source already held: `recrJqyjLPxEqPvIk`.

2. **The Carmarthen Weekly Reporter, 10 January 1896 (Supplement), p.4** — the Laugharne "Charity" column signed *"Veritas"*: *"Col[.] [a]nd Mrs Dowden, of Island House, gave [orders — the exact word is OCR-corrupt] for groceries to a large number of the poor on Christmas Eve."* This directly ties the Dowdens to **Island House**. The column is unmistakably **Laugharne**: it reports the entertainment at the town's **New Congregational Church** under **Rev Dr Wynne Jones**, and the reciters include **Abra Rowlands** and **Sarah Anne Saer** — the "Abra" forename and the "Saer" surname are both distinctively Laugharne markers already in our register. Town established *within the item*; this is contiguity, not mere page co-occurrence. Read at `newspapers.library.wales/view/3581865/3581867/22/`.

3. **The Welshman, 9 August 1895, p.8** — "Infirmary Saturday in Carmarthen and District": *"The collection at Laugharne was organised by Mrs Norton, the Castle, and was assisted by **Col, Mrs, and Miss Dowden**, Mrs Wharton, Miss Vaughan, Miss Revel…"* Town established in-item ("The collection at Laugharne"). This gives the **household** — a colonel, his wife, and at least one daughter — and their charitable standing alongside the Castle's Mrs Norton.

## 3. What this fixes

- **Residence & rank — Verified.** A Colonel Dowden lived at Island House, Wogan Street, Laugharne, c.1895–1896. Directory + newspaper + newspaper, all contemporary, all naming the same man at the same house. (Fact `recbZeSSEEoWQodE6`.)
- **Household — Verified.** The family was styled "Col, Mrs, and Miss Dowden": colonel, wife, at least one daughter; charitably active (Infirmary Saturday, August 1895; grocery relief to the poor, Christmas 1895). (Fact `recibEfYsdHOfDtTC`.)
- **Spine placement — Referenced-pending.** The occupancy fell in the house's **pre-1900, Brown's-Hotel-estate era**. The freehold was not sold until the **August 1900 auction** at Brown's Hotel, when Island House went to **Capt. Thomas Jones of Llanelly** (Doc 216). A resident colonel in 1895–96 therefore held the house by **tenancy**, not ownership. He slots cleanly into the pre-1900 tenant sequence: **Wienholt** (1863–1881, Doc 226) → **Hughes-Garbett** (to 1892, Docs 207–212) → **R. W. Pinney** (1893, Docs 214–215) → **Col. Dowden** (1895–96, this doc) → **1900 auction** → Capt. Thomas Jones. This tenancy reading is an inference from the ownership context, tiered Referenced-pending pending a lease, rate book or estate particular that names Dowden's tenure. (Fact `recBFOJWriHJLPCjy`.)

## 4. What it does NOT fix — and the traps caught

**Identity is not settled.** Neither newspaper gives an initial or a regiment, and the directory gives only "Col." Colonel Dowden's forename, regiment and precise arrival/departure dates are **not recoverable from the open Welsh Newspapers Online record** (only the two genuine Laugharne items exist, both 1895–96). This is logged as a coverage boundary, not glossed over. (Fact `recPoJ0ZMqo925LC2`.)

**Near-collisions excluded (Search Trap 3).** A plain surname search throws up three lookalikes that are **not** this man, and each is recorded so it cannot re-enter the record later:
- **"John Byng ss, Dowden"** — the master of a Bristol-Channel steamer, recurring in shipping-movement columns.
- **"W. G. Dowden"** — a works manager in Risca / Monmouthshire district-council lists.
- **"Island House, Bridgend"** — William Hopkin, Esq., a cattle-breeder (South Wales Daily News advert, 1891); a wholly different house of the same name.

## What the Cadw listing establishes (object-read)

Cadw 9653: **Grade I**, listed **30 November 1966**, Building Class **Defence**; also known as *"Laugharne Castle, Market Street"*; OS **SN 302 107**; on an elevated coastal site near the Town Hall, reached by the path in front of Castle House.

**The fabric, as described:**

- A **5-sided inner ward** of old red sandstone rubble with some freestone dressings; crenellated parapets (some ornamental) and spur buttresses.
- The **earliest surviving element** is a pair of **round towers to the north** (the NE one ruined), with a **former keep to the NW** carrying wide embrasures. The keep retains its **original conical dome**; its basement was converted to a **wine cellar in the late C18**, and the fabric received **repairs in 1920/30**.
- The towers flank a **later-C15 four-storey hall** with **late-C16 Perrot alterations** — principal rooms to the second floor, stair tower to the north.
- The Tudor set-piece is the **forward-projecting four-storey west gatehouse** (medieval core, late-C16 rebuild) carrying a fine **three-storey Tudor bay window over a pointed entrance arch with hood mould** — the architectural signature of Perrot's Renaissance conversion.
- The **curtain wall** carries a **massive first-floor fireplace** and links the gatehouse to a **three-shoot garderobe**; a **four-storey-and-basement, part-vaulted tower** stands to the SW; a **stone pent garderobe** survives on the S wall; a **ruined oriel** marks the former hall's high end, with a **medieval postern gate** beyond.
- The **east wall was demolished in the C17** (consistent with the mid-C17 slighting); a **C18 garden wall** was built on top of the surviving **late-C13 curtain wall** and continues NE to adjoin the separately listed (Grade II) **Gazebo**.
- **Interior:** a Tudor **cobbled courtyard**, a **pitched-stone kitchen floor**, and ground plans relating to all periods revealed through excavation.
- Reason for listing: **Scheduled Ancient Monument**. Cadw's own judgement: it is *"amongst the most substantial castle remains in Wales."*

**The listing's History line:** a probable **C12 earthwork** *"destroyed in 1215 by Llewelyn the Great,"* replaced by the stone castle; some **late-C15 reconstruction**; **restoration and reconstruction by Sir John Perrot 1587–1592**; *"partly destroyed in the Parliamentarian siege of 1644 under General Rowland Laugharne; subsequently pillaged for the building of town houses."*

## New datum

The listing dates **Perrot's works to 1587–1592**. That is a *works-period* figure, distinct from the two dates already in the register on the Perrot entity: the securely-attested **1575 Crown grant** (Cadw SAM 3078) and the disputed **1584** tenancy/ownership date. The **1592 terminus coincides with Perrot's attainder (17 April 1592)**. Logged Verified as the listing's own statement; the precise building chronology stays open.

## Why this target

The Globe (Globe House, Cadw 9664) is the House of Culture's own building, and the run brief keeps the desk in the town/Globe lane. The two listed buildings that physically **adjoin Globe House** — Gwalia and Manchester House — had never been read: no Entity existed for either (Airtable search, 2 Aug 2026), and neither Cadw number (9662, 9663) appears in the register's done set. They complete the streetscape the Globe faces: the block **opposite the Town Hall**, on the Market Street / Duncan Street corner. Reading them fixes the built character of the Globe's immediate setting and matches the recent cadence of paired-listing reads (Doc 317 The Limes + Moir House; HoC-306 Gainsford + Osbourne; HoC-307 Fen Hill + Fernhill).

This is a genuinely un-done, readable, open-source object squarely in the Globe lane. It is NOT an Island-House target.

### Verbatim Cadw Exterior description (shared)

> "Paired mid C19 (not shown on 1834 survey map) late Georgian rendered 3-storey fronts with cill band to double pile structure; slate roof and projecting eaves. Small-pane sash glazing, 2-windows to 2nd floor (higher and with pitched dormer roofs to Gwalia); 2-storey bay windows with cornices and later glazing to shop fronts. Bracketed hoods to doorways, 4-panel door to Manchester House."

## Findings

1. **A paired Grade II listing.** Gwalia (9663) and Manchester House (9662) are listed together, both on 25 September 1986, standing opposite the Town Hall and adjoining Globe House (the former Globe Hotel, sold 1869). [Verified — Cadw]

2. **Mid-C19 late-Georgian commercial pair.** Rendered three-storey double-pile shopfronts with cill band, slate roof and projecting eaves, small-pane sash glazing and two-storey bay windows with cornices over shop fronts. Gwalia's higher second-floor windows have pitched dormer roofs; Manchester House has a bracketed-hood four-panel door. [Verified — Cadw]

3. **Post-dated by the 1834 survey.** Cadw records the pair as "not shown on 1834 survey map." They therefore **post-date the 1834 Corporation survey of Laugharne** — a firm anchor for a mid-nineteenth-century build date. The 1834 survey map is the recurring dating instrument across the town's Cadw listings; this pair sits on the "after 1834" side of it. [Verified — Cadw]

4. **Listed for group value.** Both are classed "Commercial" and listed for group value — valued as part of the town-centre / Globe block rather than for individual architectural merit. [Verified — Cadw]

5. **"Manchester House" — a name, not yet a trade.** "Manchester House" is the stock nineteenth-century sign of a **draper's / Manchester-cotton-goods shop**. This is a naming convention only; the listing does not say this building traded as a draper's, and it is **not** asserted here as a Laugharne fact. Logged **Referenced-pending** with a cross-check route recorded (below). "Gwalia" is a literary Welsh name for Wales — a common Welsh commercial/house name of the period; noted as context, not a claim about the building's history.

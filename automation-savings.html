Why this page exists

A distinction worth drawing for the owners.

There are two kinds of automation running across the group. Some help Dominic run the operation faster behind the scenes. This page is only about the other kind — the automations that take work off the staff's plate: jobs an employee used to do by hand, now done for them so their hours go back into the guests, the floor and the room. Each one is recorded here with an honest read on the time it returns.

The Content Engine — automatic photo QA & enhancement

Live · 30 Jul 2026
Replaces manual work by: Alys, the bar manager (a non-editor) · Businesses: The Globe & Bakery
~4–6 hrsSaved per week
~230–310 hrsSaved per year
~£0Marginal cost (pennies of AI per image)

What used to happen. Social photos were handled by hand — originally by the bar manager. A busy manager, who isn't a trained photo editor, stopping between service to sort the day's phone snaps, decide which were good enough, then crop, sharpen, resize and brand each one to something postable. Slow, fiddly, and dependent on one person having the time and the eye for it.

What happens now. Staff drop a photo and walk away. The machine judges it against a fixed "premium for an upmarket restaurant" bar, screens out the weak ones, and enhances the good ones automatically — sharpened, correctly cropped, on-brand — ready for a one-tap approval. No editing skill required, no manager's time consumed.

Roughly half a shift a week, given back — indefinitely. The estimate is deliberately cautious; plug in the real posting volume and it scales straight from there.

Worth more than the hours:

  • Consistency & quality. Every image now clears the same bar and gets the same on-brand treatment, whoever took it — not just as good as the time and eye the manager could spare that day.
  • The bottleneck is gone. The work no longer waits on one person; any staff member drops a photo and the pipeline does the rest. No single point of failure.
  • A manager's time back on the floor. The hours freed are a bar manager's hours — returned to guests and running the room, not crop tools.
  • It compounds. This isn't a one-off saving; it runs every day at effectively zero cost, so the chore is off the team's plate permanently.
Guardrails. Nothing posts automatically — every image passes a human approval tick. Enhancement is honest (sharpen / crop / colour only — never fabricating the food or the venue), the original is always kept pristine, and any photo with guests but no consent is screened out and can never advance.

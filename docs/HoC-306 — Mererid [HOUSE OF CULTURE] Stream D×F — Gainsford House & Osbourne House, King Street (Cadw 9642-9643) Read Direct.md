### The finding, five lines

1. **Gainsford House (9642)** is a **later-C19 Victoria-Gothic shop-house**, Grade II, "built by the local David family, became a hotel and then a chemist in early C20" — a rare Laugharne listing that names its builders and gives a trade-use sequence, making it a Stream-F (trade) object as much as a fabric one.
2. Its front is a distinctive **2-storey, 5-window bull-nosed rubble (said to be Cornish stone)** with freestone dressings, rusticated quoins and plinth, and pointed/depressed arched window heads; the list tentatively floats the manner of **J P Seddon** — flagged "(? style of J P Seddon)," i.e. a suggestion, not an attribution.
3. **The building next door — later Laugharne's Post Office — was formerly the stores to Gainsford House**, so the two are historically one property; the Cadw entry judges the (altered) former stores "no longer of special interest."
4. **Osbourne House (9643)** is the older of the two: a **later-C18 Georgian house of 3 storeys and basement**, roughcast, **probably built as a pair with Minerva to the right**, then given Victorian alterations (Tudor hood moulds, tripartite windows, a lowered modern roof).
5. Both were listed on **25 September 1986 for GROUP VALUE only** — townscape contribution to the continuous King Street frontage row, Osbourne "stepped up from Gainsford House to left" — not individual special interest.

### What each object says

**Gainsford House — Cadw 9642, Grade II, Building Class Commercial.** Listed 25 September 1986. History: "The adjoining Post Office was formerly the stores to Gainsford House with later alterations and no longer of special interest." Exterior: "Later C19, built by the local David family, became a hotel and then a chemist in early C20. Victoria Gothic (? stype of J P Seddon) 2-storey, 5-window bull-nosed rubble (said to be cornish stone) front with freestone window dressings; rusticated quoins and plinth. Slate roof, brick and rubble chimney stacks and bracket eaves. Tripartite horned sash windows with depressed arched heads and brick banded voussoir lintels to 1st floor; steeper pointed arched heads to ground floor right with rubble voussoirs; similar treatment to central 6-panel door entrance. Shop front to left with paired semi-circular headed lights flanking pointed arched half-glazed entrance." Reason for listing: group value. Grid SN301109. (Wikidata Q29488404.)

**Osbourne House — Cadw 9643, Grade II, Building Class Domestic.** Listed 25 September 1986. Location: "Set in a continuous row of frontages stepped up from Gainsford House to left." Exterior: "Later C18 Georgian 3-storey and basement 3-window roughcast front probably built as a pair with Minerva to right; Victoria alterations. Lowered modern tiled roof, bracket eaves, brick chimney stacks. Tripartitite windows, fixed to 2nd floor, sash to 1st and ground floors; bracketed cills and, to ground floor, Tudor hood moulds. Blocked windows to 1st and 2nd floors centre over arched entrance with pedimented and bracketed hood, dentiled cornice; 6-panel door and 4-pane fanlight." Reason for listing: group value. Grid SN301109. (Wikidata Q29488405.)

### Reading notes and traps handled

- **The BLB transcription carries typos**, faithfully reproduced from the Cadw text. The Gainsford copy reads "stype" (for *style*) and "cornish" un-capitalised; the Osbourne copy reads "Tripartitite" (for *tripartite*) and "Victoria alterations" (for *Victorian*). These are transcription/OCR slips, silently corrected in the fact records and flagged in the source Notes so a later reader does not treat them as genuine variants.
- **The J P Seddon attribution is not asserted.** The list itself hedges it ("? style of"). Seddon worked extensively in west Wales, so the guess is plausible, but it is logged only as the list's own tentative note, not as a fact about the architect.
- **The exact street is Referenced-pending, not invented.** The body of the Cadw list description read this run does not name the street; "King Street" is established from the Cadw group context (the same continuous terrace as the King-Street Exeter/Pines/Elm trio and Redford House / Minerva) and from postcode/property data (SA33 4QE, King Street). A direct read of the FullReport Location field would promote it to Verified — recorded as the immediate follow-up.
- **No Dylan or Globe hook was manufactured** and none exists in these records; the pair is town fabric read on its own terms (per the Doc-140 register). Gainsford's builder-and-trade sequence (David family → hotel → chemist) is a genuine Stream-F trade thread in its own right, independent of the Globe.

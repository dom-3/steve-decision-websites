### Why this run exists

The Pigot 1830 Laugharne entry was already read once and logged as **Doc 228** (entity `recFTnbf9YfzRv2bp`), but that pass captured only the **trade rosters** — victuallers, food and provision trades, building and craft trades, clothing and leather trades, professions/services/officers, and carriage. The directory's own **"Gentry and Clergy"** heading — the town's resident gentry, clergy and half-pay officers c.1830 — was never logged. Doc 229 did the equivalent work for **Pigot 1844**, so both Pigot years are otherwise fully mined and a 1830→1844 change comparison already exists (fact `recMGfvbPnO70xNkF`).

This run closes the one un-mined slice of the 1830 object: the social-standing roster. It is additive enrichment of a source already in the register, not a new claim on a fresh target.

### The near-collision guard applied

Pigot lists St Clears and Laugharne **together** under each heading, every name place-tagged. The "Gentry and Clergy" list mixes both towns in a single alphabetical block. Only names explicitly tagged **"; Laugharne"** are attributed here; the co-listed St Clears gentry (Rice Beynon, Reece Edwards esq, the three Rev. Evanses, Mrs Ann Jones, Rev James Philipps, Capt. John Price, Capt. Edward Scott, Rev David Thos Thomas, John Trehearn esq, Mrs Margaret Waters, Lt Thos Weir) are excluded. This is the standing page-level/near-collision discipline applied in directory form: co-listing under one heading is not evidence of Laugharne residence — the item's own place-tag is.

### Finding 1 — the 1830 Laugharne gentry & clergy roster (Verified)

Pigot's 1830 Directory names 21 resident gentry and clergy entries at Laugharne (fact `recii2gNznohWE3oo`; "the Misses Thomas" is a single plural entry):

- **Gentry / esquires & gentlemen:** Dennis Fairchild esq; Joseph Howells gent; Henry Sampson Locke gent; Timothy Powell esq; George Stackpole esq; John Uniack esq; Thomas Waters esq; John Whitaker gent; Henry Williams esq (of Gosport, Laugharne).
- **Ladies (widows / unmarried heads of household):** Mrs William Callow; Mrs Alice Evans; Mrs Elizabeth Starke; the Misses Thomas; Mrs Sarah Trehearn; Miss Van Heythuysen.
- **Clergy:** Rev Augustus Lodowick Hamilton; Rev George Parry.
- **Retired / half-pay officers:** Col. John Brown; Col. Rowland Edwards; Capt. Henry Hill; Capt. William Larn.

Tiered **Verified** (attested in a named printed directory via a faithful transcription), not Verified-strong — the facsimile page-image itself has not yet been read, only Gareth Hicks's transcription of it.

### Finding 2 — Mrs Elizabeth Starke of Castle House corroborated (Referenced-pending)

The headline result. Among the 1830 ladies is **"Starke, Mrs Elizabeth; Laugharne."** The register already documents the **Starke family of Castle House** in depth (entities `recjHV5p3gt0s0Aqv`, `recYwgaUC3Leb5puE`, `recRcaKjzhOrUFvNU`, `recrgzBn1c3y7HPwb`):

- **Elizabeth Ravenscroft** inherited Laugharne Castle in 1787 under the will of her grandfather Pennoyre Watkins, and in **1798 married Col. Richard Isaac Starke** — founding the Starke tenure of Castle House and the castle.
- The Starkes owned Castle House and the castle in common for some **175 years, 1798–1973**, laid out the ruins as a picturesque garden, and modified Castle House in the Regency phase **c.1810**.
- The family's ownership ran to **Miss Anne Starke**, who placed the castle in the guardianship of the Secretary of State for Wales in **1973**.

Pigot's "Mrs Elizabeth Starke" is, on the exact forename match plus the register's own established Starke chronology, **very probably Elizabeth Ravenscroft-Starke** — placing the castle-owning family resident in the town in 1830, in the same generation as the c.1810 Castle House remodelling (fact `reci3NKLJiSDZehNv`). Held **Referenced-pending**, not upgraded: the directory names no seat and gives no genealogy, so this is a strong onomastic-and-chronological inference, not a directory-stated identification. It should not be promoted without a seat/probate confirmation (e.g. the 1846 tithe apportionment for the Castle House plot, or an Elizabeth Starke will).

### Finding 3 — a Hamilton professional dynasty in view (Verified)

The two 1830 clergy are the **Rev Augustus Lodowick Hamilton** and the **Rev George Parry** (fact `recK0M0NV9zpRqoZP`). The **Hamilton** surname recurs across the town's professional class in the already-logged rosters: the 1830 surgeons "Hawkins & Hamilton", and in 1844 the attorney **W. S. Hamilton** and surgeon **Henry Hamilton** (Doc 229). The continuity is suggestive of a resident professional family; incumbency titles and dates are not established here.

### Finding 4 — a genteel military/maritime town (Verified, with one guarded lead)

Four retired/half-pay officers sit among the 1830 gentry — **Col. John Brown, Col. Rowland Edwards, Capt. Henry Hill, Capt. William Larn** (fact `recv2KdHRiZW5cj1i`) — consistent with a small genteel maritime and military town. Two guardrails:

- Whether **"Col. John Brown"** relates to the family behind **Brown's Hotel, King Street** (Cadw 9638, Doc 194) is **UNSOURCED**. "Brown" is a common name and the directory states no connection; logged only as a lead, not a link.
- **"Capt. William Larn"** echoing the directory's own note that Laugharne is "commonly called Larne" is **coincidental** — a surname, not a town-name gloss. (Standing guardrail: never gloss the town-name.)

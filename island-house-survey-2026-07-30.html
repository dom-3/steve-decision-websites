This is the first full pass of the site — 53 stations across three areas: the vaulted undercroft store (being refurbished), the historic courtyard rooms, and the new blockwork service/plant zone at low level. Most of the building is progressing soundly. Two things, however, need your attention now.

What matters this week

  • A whole zone of new blockwork rooms is visibly wet. Stations 43–50, 52 and 53 show white salt efflorescence, green algae and water running down the block, with standing water on the floor in several rooms. The cause of the water must be found and fixed before these walls are lined, insulated or finished — otherwise the finishes will fail and money is wasted. This is the single most urgent cluster.
  • Temporary works are holding parts of the building up. Timber shoring is carrying beams at Station 12, and adjustable steel (acrow) props are supporting the new red steel arch ribs at Stations 47–48. These are load-bearing and must not be disturbed until a structural engineer has confirmed the propping.

Rubble stone walls, curved timber ceilings, fireplaces, a stone slit window, massive timber lintels and plank doors are all currently visible (Stations 1, 5–9, 16, 24–27, 36–37). Once plaster and finishes go on, they are hidden. This is a recording opportunity that will not come again.
  • New block is built hard against old stone in many places (Stations 21, 29, 30, 31, 34, 39) — these junctions need a conservation-aware detail so damp is not trapped in the historic walls.

6The Damp & Timber Specialist

🔴 Red Active, persistent moisture across the new blockwork zone — the defining risk on this building. Find and fix before lining.

Since last week: this is the Week 1 baseline — first moisture walk. All readings below are visual signals only, to be confirmed with a moisture meter and opening-up on site.

The moisture story

The new blockwork service/plant zone (Stations 41–53) shows a consistent picture of active, ongoing wetting: white salt efflorescence and green/black algae streaking down the blockwork, and standing water/puddles on several floor slabs. Efflorescence means water is repeatedly moving through the blocks and evaporating at the face, carrying salts with it; algae and standing water mean the wetness is persistent, not a one-off. Worst affected: Stations 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50, 52 and 53 (High). Early/moderate signs at Stations 41, 42 and 51 (Medium) — treat as part of the same zone.

Likely mechanisms — to determine on site

  • Penetrating / groundwater: the zone is at low level with an open excavation/drainage trench alongside (Stations 49, 51). Strongly suggests ground water or lack of tanking/DPM against the blockwork — to confirm.
  • Rising damp: standing water on slabs (Stations 46, 47, 52) points to slab DPM/falls to check.
  • Condensation: enclosed, unventilated cells (Stations 45, 52) may add a condensation component — assess ventilation.
  • Separately, low-level render staining on undercroft piers (Station 1) to check for rising damp before render is completed.

Penetrating vs rising vs condensation must be separated by moisture metering, and the water source traced (ground levels, drainage, tanking) before any conclusion.

Breathability caution

  • Where new block abuts historic rubble stone (Stations 21, 29, 30, 31, 34, 39), no non-breathable / cementitious finishes that would trap moisture in the old fabric. Repairs to historic walls should be lime-based and vapour-open.
  • Retained historic rubble base showing spalling (Station 31) is a decay/damp signal at the old/new junction — investigate before it is enclosed.

Timber

  • Massive historic bressumer/lintel over the thick-wall passage (Station 8) shows possible staining/decay — needs a timber survey for rot and beetle before it is covered.
  • Keep new stair timber, salvaged timber and any insulation clear of the wet zone until it is dry.

Actions

  • [OWNER DECISION] Instruct a damp specialist to meter and diagnose the wet zone (Stations 43–53); find and fix the water source before any lining/insulation.
  • Check slab DPM and falls (Stations 46, 47, 52); ventilate/dry enclosed cells (Stations 45, 52) meanwhile.
  • Specify breathable/lime repairs at all old/new junctions; timber survey at Station 8; meter Station 1 piers before render.
